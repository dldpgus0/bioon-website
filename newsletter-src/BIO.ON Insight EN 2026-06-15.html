🔤 Word of the Week

In Korean: 장기지속형

Long-acting

The key concept behind this week’s once-weekly oral HIV pill (Story 3) and K-bio obesity drugs (Story 2)

Long-acting describes a formulation or molecular strategy designed so a drug is released slowly or stays in the body for a long time. The norm used to be one pill a day or a daily injection — long-acting drugs stretch dosing out to once a week or once a month. This week’s once-weekly oral HIV treatment ISL/LEN from Gilead and Merck and Korean companies’ once-monthly obesity drug pipelines are both riding this trend.

💡 Analogy: Energy that only lasts if you eat every day vs. a single slow-release supplement capsule. Fast-absorbed drugs act immediately but must be taken daily. Long-acting drugs are designed like ‘a candle that melts little by little’ inside the body, covering a week or a month with one dose.

Short-acting

Daily HIV medicines (such as Biktarvy) and weekly semaglutide injections are typical examples. They’re reliable, but their effect drops when adherence (compliance) slips.

Long-acting

This week’s ISL/LEN (once-weekly oral) and Peptron’s PT403 and Inventage Lab’s IVL3021 (once-monthly obesity injections) fall here. Easier dosing makes it more likely to translate into real-world treatment benefit, and it’s a point of market differentiation.

📰 This Week’s Top 3 Stories

Story 1 · Big Pharma M&A

GSK Buys Nuvalent for $10.6B (About ₩14 Trillion) — Securing a Next-Generation Lung Cancer Targeted Therapy Pipeline in One Move

Source · GSK press release / Nuvalent (Nasdaq: NUVL) | Jun 9, 2026 · expected to close in Q3

Key Facts

GSK agreed to acquire Nuvalent, a Boston clinical-stage precision oncology company, for $124 per share, $10.6B in total (a 40% premium to the previous close). There are two key assets — zidesamtinib, a next-generation ROS1 inhibitor with a PDUFA date of September 18, and neladalkib, a next-generation ALK inhibitor with a PDUFA date of November 27. Both are under FDA review for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and hold Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations. It’s GSK’s third big acquisition of 2026, part of a strategy to fill its oncology pipeline quickly ahead of the 2028 patent expiry of its core HIV products.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

The ‘premium’ Big Pharma pays to acquire a start-up is a bet on the future value of its pipeline. A 40% premium means paying 40% more than the current market price — GSK judged that zidesamtinib and neladalkib could create far more value if approved. The weakness of existing ROS1 and ALK inhibitors is resistance; Nuvalent’s next-generation versions work against resistance mutations and have high penetration into brain metastases.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

ROS1- and ALK-positive patients make up only 1–3% of NSCLC in Korea, but if zidesamtinib and neladalkib are approved, talks on bringing them to Korea will begin in earnest. GSK’s run of big acquisitions shows how actively Big Pharma uses M&A to deal with patent cliffs — and it’s a signal that Big Pharma demand remains strong in the licensing and tech-transfer talks of Korean companies such as Yuhan and Hanmi.

Story 2 · Clinical Data

K-Bio at ADA 2026: Beyond Weight to Muscle, Heart and Liver Protection — Data That Cracked Novo and Lilly’s Duopoly

Source · The Fact, Etoday / ADA 2026, New Orleans | Jun 5–8, 2026

Key Facts

At ADA 2026 in New Orleans (June 5–8), Korean companies including Hanmi, Dong-A ST, HK inno.N, Peptron and Inventage Lab turned out in force. The core theme was “beyond GLP-1.” Hanmi’s HM17321 (a myostatin inhibitor) cut fat while increasing muscle mass and also showed kidney protection; Dong-A ST and MetaVia’s DA-1726 (a GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist) produced an average 9.1% weight loss in 54 days. HK inno.N’s ecnoglutide (a cAMP-biased GLP-1) delivered 35%+ greater weight loss than semaglutide at 20 weeks. The shared strategy: stand apart from Novo and Lilly with ‘weight loss + organ protection + long-acting formulations.’

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Today’s number one and two obesity drugs are Novo Nordisk’s Wegovy and Eli Lilly’s Zepbound. Since it’s hard for K-bio to compete on ‘the same weight loss,’ companies are differentiating by cutting fat while preserving muscle (HM17321), protecting the heart, liver and kidneys too (DA-1726), or improving convenience with once-monthly injections (Peptron PT403, Inventage Lab IVL3021). The area most likely to lead to licensing deals is this ‘better body composition + organ protection’ combination.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

With Korean reimbursement for obesity drugs under discussion, the ADA data signals that ‘K-bio can produce globally competitive data too.’ Obesity and metabolic disease is one of the fastest-growing segments through 2030, and demand is rising for clinical, BD and regulatory roles alike. Following the clinical progress of the companies that presented at ADA will help with both interviews and career exploration.

📌 Hanmi HM17321 (myostatin inhibition: muscle up, fat down), Dong-A ST DA-1726 (9.1% loss in 54 days) and HK inno.N ecnoglutide (35%+ better than semaglutide) were Korea’s headline results at ADA 2026. — Compiled from The Fact and Etoday

Story 3 · Partnership

Gilead and Merck Win Two Phase 3 Trials for a Once-Weekly Oral HIV Pill — Turning ‘Every Day’ Into ‘Once a Week’

Source · Merck (MSD) press release / Gilead Sciences | ISLEND-1 & ISLEND-2 Phase 3 | Jun 8, 2026

Key Facts

The once-weekly oral combination islatravir/lenacapavir (ISL/LEN), co-developed by Gilead and Merck (MSD), met its primary endpoint in both Phase 3 trials, ISLEND-1 and ISLEND-2. ISLEND-1 showed 48-week non-inferiority versus daily Biktarvy, and ISLEND-2 versus standard therapy. If approved, it would be the world’s first long-acting oral HIV treatment. Islatravir (Merck) is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase translocation inhibitor, and lenacapavir (Gilead) is a first-in-class capsid inhibitor with no cross-resistance to existing HIV drugs. It’s a collaboration where two Big Pharmas combined their strengths.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

The standard in HIV treatment is a combination pill that blocks several steps of the virus’s replication cycle at once. Until now that meant one pill a day; ISL/LEN is one pill a week. Lenacapavir (a capsid inhibitor) works through a completely new mechanism, attacking the ‘capsid’ protein shell of HIV, so it also works on viruses resistant to older drugs. If existing drugs jam the virus’s ‘machinery,’ lenacapavir locks ‘the box the virus comes in’.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

Less daily pill burden means better adherence, which changes the whole paradigm of HIV care. For Korean CMOs and clinical CROs, demand for long-acting formulation manufacturing is growing too. And after GSK’s HIV patents expire in 2028, it’s worth watching how ISL/LEN fills the gap — and whether that competition reaches Korea’s HIV treatment market.

📌 Once-weekly oral ISL/LEN met 48-week non-inferiority in Phase 3 (ISLEND-1, ISLEND-2). Built on the capsid inhibitor lenacapavir; if approved, the first long-acting oral HIV treatment. — Summary of Merck/Gilead announcement

🔬 This Week’s Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Why Big Pharma Is Buying This Now

Why GSK spent $10.6B —
the equation behind an M&A premium

① How a Big Pharma M&A premium is calculated

When Big Pharma sets an acquisition premium, it looks at three main things. First, the pipeline’s market potential (peak sales) — could zidesamtinib and neladalkib generate billions a year if approved? Second, probability of success (PoS) — if they’re already under FDA review, Phase 1 and 2 risk is behind them and uncertainty drops sharply. Third, remaining patent life — how many years of exclusive sales after approval? GSK judged that, multiplied together, these three made the company worth 40% more than its current share price. On top of that, Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations mean faster review plus tax incentives, raising the acquisition value further.

③ Why now — the pressure of the patent cliff

GSK’s core HIV medicines lose patent protection in mid-2028. Once generic competition starts, revenue falls sharply. Offsetting that requires new blockbusters — and buying a clinically proven pipeline is faster and surer than developing one in-house. Global biotech M&A in 2026 has already reached 201 deals worth $106B, the fastest pace since the pandemic. For K-bio, this trend is an opportunity — the more Big Pharmas look to buy pipelines like GSK did, the more Korean companies with proven clinical data can become targets.

④ Career takeaway — what to prepare for in this M&A wave

✓ Skill to build

If you’re aiming for BD (business development) or licensing, you need to understand the logic of an M&A premium (peak sales × PoS × patent life). In clinical roles, know the PDUFA process and the Breakthrough Therapy pathway inside out. If you work in K-bio, practising analysing your own company’s data from the view of ‘How would GSK see our pipeline?’ will set you apart in interviews and on the job.

⚠ Trap

Assuming ‘if Big Pharma buys, it must be good’ is risky. Even GSK’s 2026 EPS will be slightly diluted. An acquisition premium pays for the future up front, so a bad PDUFA result means big losses. The habit of reading not just ‘how much was paid’ but ‘why now, and why at this price’ when you read M&A news is key in BD, investment and regulatory careers alike.

What this week’s three stories share is that “innovation isn’t finished alone.” GSK spent $10.6B to buy ten years of Nuvalent’s R&D, K-bio proved itself with data on the ADA stage, and Gilead and Merck combined each other’s drugs to turn ‘every day’ into ‘once a week.’ Buy, prove, partner — we saw all three routes of biotech innovation in one week.

🔤 Word of the Week

Warburg Effect

The mechanism behind this week's BPGbio story (Story 2)

When oxygen is available, normal cells make energy efficiently in their mitochondria through oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS). Cancer cells, oddly, lean on the far less efficient glycolysis even when there's plenty of oxygen. It's named after Otto Warburg, who described it in the 1920s. It's the result of cancer cells rewiring their metabolism to grow fast.

💡 Analogy: The motorway (mitochondria) is wide open, but cancer cells insist on taking the narrow back streets (glycolysis). BPM31510 tries to push them back onto the motorway (OXPHOS), and the oxidative stress from that switch is what brings them down.

Glycolysis — what cancer cells prefer

Breaks glucose down to lactate quickly, even with oxygen around. Poor energy yield, but it supplies building blocks fast. Tumours that rely on this heavily are called "Warburg-avid."

OXPHOS — normal metabolism

Uses oxygen in the mitochondria to make energy efficiently. BPM31510 aims to shift tumour metabolism back here, triggering ROS and BCL-2-dependent apoptosis and making tumours more sensitive to radiation.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · REGULATORY FILING

Zenas Files obexelimab for IgG4-Related Disease After a 56% Cut in Relapse Risk

GlobeNewswire · Zenas BioPharma (Nasdaq: ZBIO) | May 28, 2026

Key Facts

Zenas BioPharma submitted a Biologics License Application (BLA) to the FDA for obexelimab in IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD). In the Phase 3 INDIGO trial, obexelimab cut the risk of flares by 56% versus placebo over 52 weeks (hazard ratio 0.44, p=0.0005) and met all four key secondary endpoints. It's a bifunctional monoclonal antibody that binds CD19 and FcγRIIb at the same time, dialling down B-cell activity without depleting the cells.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

IgG4-RD is a rare chronic disease where immune cells attack several organs, causing fibrosis and damage. Existing B-cell drugs like rituximab wipe out B cells altogether. obexelimab leaves the B cells alive and just flips their switch off, which is how it hopes to stand out on side effects such as infection risk.

🌍 Global Lens

IgG4-RD was first described in Japan, and doctors in East Asia have some of the deepest experience treating it, so data and filings there will matter. obexelimab also won't have the field to itself: Amgen's Uplizna was approved by the FDA for IgG4-RD in 2025. To stand out, it will need to win on safety and convenience, not novelty alone.

Why it matters for your career: RA teams spend their days judging whether evidence will hold up with a regulator. Showing you can size up a data package quickly sets you apart.

📌 "Based on the meaningful clinical effect and favourable safety, we believe obexelimab can play an important role as a first-line treatment for IgG4-RD." — Lisa von Moltke, Chief Medical Officer, Zenas

STORY 2 · DRUG DEVELOPMENT · ONCOLOGY

BPGbio Brings Its Mitochondria-Targeting Glioblastoma Drug BPM31510 to ASCO 2026

GlobeNewswire · BPGbio | May 28, 2026

Key Facts

BPGbio said it would present a trial-in-progress update on the Phase 2 study of BPM31510 in newly diagnosed glioblastoma (GBM) at ASCO 2026 (abstract TPS2101, presented June 1 by Stanford's Dr Seema Nagpal). BPM31510 is a first-in-class lipid-nanoparticle formulation of oxidised coenzyme Q10, given with vitamin K on top of standard care (radiation plus temozolomide). In Phase 1, PET imaging showed a reversal of the Warburg effect, from glycolysis back to OXPHOS.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Glioblastoma is the most aggressive adult brain cancer, with very low five-year survival and almost no new drugs in decades. Rather than killing cancer cells directly, BPM31510 pushes their abnormal metabolism back toward normal so they break down on their own. The Word of the Week above explains the mechanism.

🌍 Global Lens

Glioblastoma trials are small and slow almost everywhere, so academic networks in the US, UK and Europe often pool patients just to reach useful numbers. A trial-in-progress abstract won't move any market. What it does show is that metabolism-based approaches are still getting serious clinical attention, and that's a useful signal if you're tracking where neuro-oncology money goes.

Why it matters for your career: Translational roles are about linking a mechanism to something you can measure in patients. Biomarker evidence like this is exactly what they work with.

📌 "Phase 1 showed reversal of the Warburg effect on PET, which could finally unlock the mitochondrial vulnerability of glioblastoma." — Vivek Subbiah, Stanford Cancer Institute

STORY 3 · MARKETS · WARNING SIGN

Hoth Therapeutics Rebrands as an AI and Space Company, and the Stock Takes Off

Channel NewsAsia (Reuters) · Rocket One (Nasdaq: RKTO) | May 28, 2026

Key Facts

Hoth Therapeutics, which had been developing skin disease drugs, renamed itself Rocket One and pivoted to AI infrastructure, chips and space-based computing. On its first day under the new ticker (NASDAQ: RKTO), shares jumped about 60% in pre-market trading. The company says it will focus on space-based AI systems for satellites and nanomagnetic AI chips. Its Q1 net loss was more than $2.6 million, it had no operating revenue, and its market value was around $26 million. The biotech business stays on as a separate subsidiary.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

This is a textbook case of a struggling small-cap swapping its whole story to attract investors, riding the AI-and-space excitement around SpaceX's IPO filing. No revenue, no product yet, and the stock jumped on the name alone. That's a clear warning sign.

🌍 Global Lens

Buzzword rebrands aren't new or American. During the 2017–18 crypto boom, companies around the world added "blockchain" to their names and watched their shares jump, and the SEC ended up warning investors about exactly that pattern. For biotech, the sadder signal is how tight small-cap funding has become: some companies would rather drop the science than keep raising money for it.

Why it matters for your career: Checking whether a company has revenue, cash and a real product before a headline excites you is basic due diligence. Investor relations, equity research and BD teams expect that habit from day one.

📌 Companies "change their whole narrative" to pull in investors — and shareholders carry that much more risk. (Analyst comment in the article)

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · What Counts as Value

Data vs narrative —
where does a biotech prove its worth?

① Three ways to "prove" value in one week

This week's stories make a neat contrast. Zenas proved its value with a 52-week randomised trial (56% fewer relapses, p=0.0005). BPGbio built scientific support with a mechanism confirmed on PET and an ASCO presentation. Rocket One had no product and no revenue, and its stock rose 60% on a new name and a new story. All three got attention, but the evidence behind each was on a completely different level.

③ Why the distinction matters

Trust in biotech ultimately comes from data that can be reproduced. Anyone can check a p-value, a hazard ratio or a mechanism, but a narrative fades as soon as the market's mood does. Short-term prices can move on stories. Long-term value is built by trial data, approvals and revenue.

④ Career takeaway: the question that travels

✓ Skill to build

The habit this week rewards is simple: ask "what's the evidence?" before you ask "is this exciting?" It works when you're reading a trial readout, sizing up a company you might join, or preparing a case for an interview. People in RA, clinical development and equity research do this all day, and candidates who already do it stand out in any biotech role.

⚠ Trap

Judging a company, or a potential employer, by its theme. Check the pipeline's clinical stage, the revenue and the evidence for the mechanism yourself. It's the same homework for job-seekers and investors.

💡 Editor's Insight

From the headlines alone, all three companies looked like they were winning this week. Peel back one layer and one is holding a p-value, one a PET scan, and one a new company name. Next time a biotech story lands in your feed, ask "so what's the evidence?" That one question is what trains your eye for this industry. See you next issue. 🙏

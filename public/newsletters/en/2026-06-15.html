🔤 Word of the Week

Long-acting

The idea behind this week's weekly HIV pill (Story 3) and Korean obesity drugs (Story 2)

A long-acting drug is designed, through its formulation or the molecule itself, to release slowly or stay in the body for a long time. Daily pills and daily injections used to be the norm; long-acting drugs stretch dosing to once a week or once a month. Gilead and Merck's once-weekly oral ISL/LEN for HIV and Korean companies' once-monthly obesity injections are both riding this trend.

💡 Analogy: A fast-acting drug is like a snack: quick energy, but you need another one soon. A long-acting drug is more like a slow-burning candle, releasing a little at a time so one dose covers a week or a month.

Short-acting — frequent dosing

Daily HIV pills like Biktarvy are the classic example. They work well, but if patients miss doses, the drug stops working as well — adherence is the weak spot.

Long-acting — dose less often

This week's weekly oral ISL/LEN and monthly obesity injections (Peptron's PT403, Inventage Lab's IVL3021). Easier dosing makes it more likely the treatment works in real life, and it's a way to stand out in the market.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · BIG PHARMA M&A

GSK Buys Nuvalent for $10.6B, Picking Up Two Next-Generation Lung Cancer Drugs at Once

GSK press release / Nuvalent (Nasdaq: NUVL) | Jun 9, 2026 · expected to close in Q3

Key Facts

GSK agreed to buy Boston-based precision oncology company Nuvalent for $124 a share, about $10.6 billion in total and a 40% premium to the last closing price. There are two key assets: zidesamtinib, a next-generation ROS1 inhibitor with a PDUFA date of September 18, and neladalkib, a next-generation ALK inhibitor with a PDUFA date of November 27. Both are under FDA review for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with breakthrough and orphan designations. It's GSK's third big acquisition of 2026, as it races to fill its oncology pipeline before a key HIV patent expires in 2028.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

When Big Pharma buys a smaller company, the premium is a bet on future pipeline value. A 40% premium means paying 40% above the market price because GSK believes zidesamtinib and neladalkib will be worth far more once approved. Older ROS1 and ALK inhibitors tend to stop working as tumours develop resistance. Nuvalent's versions are designed to work against resistance mutations and to get into the brain, where lung cancer often spreads.

🌍 Global Lens

A British company buying in Boston is now the standard way UK and European pharma fill their pipelines: the innovation is often American, and the buyer often isn't. US approval comes first. For patients in the UK and Europe, the next hurdles are MHRA and EMA reviews and then NICE's cost-effectiveness verdict, which can decide how quickly these drugs actually reach clinics.

Why it matters for your career: BD roles expect you to reason about value rather than repeat the headline number: why a 40% premium, why now, and what the patent clock is forcing.

STORY 2 · CLINICAL DATA · OBESITY

Korean Biotechs at ADA 2026: Beyond Weight Loss to Muscle, Heart and Liver

The Fact · Etoday / ADA 2026, New Orleans | Jun 5–8, 2026

Key Facts

Hanmi, Dong-A ST, HK inno.N, Peptron and Inventage Lab were among the Korean companies at ADA 2026 in New Orleans (June 5–8), and the theme was "beyond GLP-1." Hanmi's HM17321 (a myostatin inhibitor) cut fat while increasing muscle mass and showed kidney protection. Dong-A ST and MetaVia's DA-1726 (a GLP-1/glucagon dual agonist) produced 9.1% average weight loss in 54 days. HK inno.N's ecnoglutide (a cAMP-biased GLP-1) showed more than 35% greater weight loss than semaglutide at 20 weeks. The shared strategy: weight loss plus organ protection plus long-acting formulations, to stand apart from Novo and Lilly.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

The market leaders are Novo Nordisk (Wegovy) and Eli Lilly (Zepbound). Matching them on weight loss alone is a tough fight, so Korean companies are differentiating: keeping muscle while losing fat (HM17321), protecting heart, liver and kidneys (DA-1726), or making dosing easier with monthly injections (Peptron's PT403, Inventage Lab's IVL3021). The body-composition-plus-organ-protection angle is where licensing interest is most likely.

🌍 Global Lens

Seen from outside Korea, this is a country pitching itself as the next source of obesity assets after the US and Denmark. Biotechs in China and Europe are making the same "beyond weight loss" pitch, so the licensing race for muscle-sparing and organ-protecting drugs is truly international. Showing data at a US congress is how these assets get onto Big Pharma's radar.

Why it matters for your career: Obesity teams are growing across clinical, BD and regulatory roles. Knowing where the field is heading, not just who leads it, makes you a much more interesting candidate.

📌 Korea's ADA 2026 highlights: Hanmi's HM17321 (more muscle, less fat), Dong-A ST's DA-1726 (9.1% in 54 days) and HK inno.N's ecnoglutide (35%+ better than semaglutide). — The Fact · Etoday roundup

STORY 3 · PARTNERSHIP · HIV

Gilead and Merck's Once-Weekly HIV Pill Wins Two Phase 3 Trials

Merck (MSD) press release / Gilead Sciences | ISLEND-1 and ISLEND-2 Phase 3 | Jun 8, 2026

Key Facts

Gilead and Merck (MSD)'s co-developed once-weekly oral combination, islatravir/lenacapavir (ISL/LEN), met its primary endpoints in both Phase 3 trials. ISLEND-1 showed non-inferiority to daily Biktarvy, and ISLEND-2 to standard therapy, at 48 weeks. If approved, it would be the first long-acting oral HIV treatment. Islatravir (Merck) is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase translocation inhibitor; lenacapavir (Gilead) is a first-in-class capsid inhibitor with no cross-resistance to existing HIV drugs. Two Big Pharma companies combined their strengths.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

HIV treatment is usually a combination pill that blocks several steps of the virus's life cycle. Until now, that meant one pill a day. ISL/LEN is one pill a week. Lenacapavir attacks the capsid, the protein shell around the virus's genetic material, so it works even against viruses resistant to older drugs. If older drugs jam the virus's machinery, lenacapavir locks the box the virus travels in.

🌍 Global Lens

The market that matters most for long-acting HIV drugs isn't the richest one: sub-Saharan Africa carries most of the world's HIV burden. Gilead has already signed licences so generic makers can supply lenacapavir for prevention in low- and middle-income countries. Whether a weekly treatment pill follows a similar path will decide how much it changes the epidemic, not just the US market.

Why it matters for your career: Co-development deals need alliance managers, joint clinical teams and formulation experts. It's a less obvious career path, and a growing one.

📌 Once-weekly oral ISL/LEN met 48-week non-inferiority in ISLEND-1 and ISLEND-2. Built on the capsid inhibitor lenacapavir, it would be the first long-acting oral HIV treatment if approved. — Merck / Gilead announcement summary

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Why Big Pharma Is Buying Now

Why GSK paid $10.6 billion —
the maths behind an M&A premium

① How a Big Pharma premium gets calculated

Three things drive the price. First, peak sales: could zidesamtinib and neladalkib each earn billions a year once approved? Second, probability of success: drugs already under FDA review have skipped past most of the uncertainty. Third, remaining patent life: how many years of exclusive sales are left after approval? GSK multiplied those together and decided the answer was worth 40% more than the market price. Breakthrough and orphan designations add value too, with faster reviews and tax incentives.

③ Why now — the patent cliff

GSK's key HIV medicines lose patent protection in mid-2028, and sales drop fast once competition arrives. Replacing that takes new blockbusters, and buying a clinically proven pipeline is faster and surer than building one. Global biotech M&A in 2026 had already reached 201 deals worth $106 billion, the fastest pace since the pandemic. For smaller biotechs, that's an opportunity: the more Big Pharma wants to buy, the more a company with solid clinical data becomes a target.

④ Career takeaway: think like the buyer

✓ Skill to build

All three stories reward the same muscle: looking at an asset the way a buyer or partner would. What's its peak sales potential, how risky is it, and how long is it protected? Whether you're in BD, clinical development or at a small biotech, practising that lens on your own company's pipeline is great interview prep, and it's exactly how BD and corporate strategy teams think every day.

⚠ Trap

Assuming a Big Pharma acquisition is automatically good news. Even GSK expects a small dilution to 2026 earnings. A premium is paying for the future in advance, and a bad PDUFA outcome turns it into a big loss. When you read M&A news, ask "why now, and why this price?", not just "how much?"

💡 Editor's Insight

Writing this issue, what stuck with me is that innovation rarely finishes on its own. Before one good drug reaches patients, someone has to fund it, someone has to generate the data, and someone has to decide to combine their molecule with a rival's. Next time a big deal hits the news, before looking at the price, ask "which road did this company choose?" The same number will read very differently. See you next issue. 🙏

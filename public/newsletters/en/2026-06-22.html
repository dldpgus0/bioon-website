🔤 Word of the Week

Amylin Analogue

The second ingredient in this week's Novo Nordisk CagriSema story (Story 2)

Amylin is a hormone released by the pancreas's beta cells alongside insulin. It makes you feel full after a meal, slows stomach emptying and suppresses glucagon, which keeps blood sugar from spiking. People with type 2 diabetes produce less amylin as well as less insulin. Cagrilintide mimics amylin. In CagriSema it's paired with semaglutide (a GLP-1 drug), so two fullness pathways are switched on at once, for stronger effects on blood sugar and weight.

💡 Analogy: Giving someone with type 2 diabetes semaglutide alone is like rowing a two-oar boat with one oar. Without amylin, half the fullness signal is missing. CagriSema hands over both oars — GLP-1 and the amylin analogue — so the same effort moves you further and more steadily.

GLP-1 alone — single agonist

Semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) is the classic example. It activates the GLP-1 receptor to boost fullness and insulin release. Proven, but with the amylin pathway left empty, there's a ceiling on what it can do.

GLP-1 + amylin analogue — combination

This week's CagriSema (cagrilintide plus semaglutide). It stimulates both fullness signals, tackling blood sugar (HbA1c) and weight together, and beat semaglutide alone in the REIMAGINE Phase 3 programme.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · AI DRUG DISCOVERY

Insilico Medicine and SK Biopharm Bet up to $2.5B on AI-Designed Brain and Immune Drugs

Fierce Biotech / Insilico Medicine · SK Biopharm | Jun 22, 2026

Key Facts

AI drug discovery company Insilico Medicine signed a partnership with SK Biopharm worth up to $2.5 billion. The deal focuses on discovering candidates for neuroimmune and central nervous system (CNS) diseases, with payments tied to milestones. Insilico's generative AI platform analyses a target protein's 3D structure and designs the best candidates from millions of virtual molecules, compressing years of early discovery into weeks. SK Biopharm brings neuroscience expertise and handles clinical development and commercialisation.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Traditional discovery means chemists making and testing thousands of compounds, hoping to get lucky. AI design flips it: give the AI the target's structure and it works backwards to a molecule that fits, like a key cut for a specific lock. It's the difference between a paper map and a sat-nav. The catch: a molecule that looks great on a computer still has to work in people, and that clinical bottleneck hasn't gone away.

🎓 Career Lens

Most relevant for: Computational Chemistry, Bioinformatics / ML, Business Development

Interviewing for an AI drug discovery role? You'll almost certainly be asked where AI really helps. A grounded answer:

"AI is clearly speeding up the front end, going from a target to candidate molecules in weeks instead of years. That's what the Insilico and SK Biopharm deal is paying for. But most of the $2.5 billion is milestones, and CNS drugs fail in the clinic a lot, so the real test is whether these molecules survive human trials."

Why it matters: AI drug discovery teams are hiring computational chemists, bioinformaticians and ML engineers, and they value candidates who know where the hype ends.

STORY 2 · PHASE 3 · METABOLIC

Novo Nordisk's CagriSema Improves Blood Sugar and Weight Together in the REIMAGINE Phase 3

Novo Nordisk press release (PR Newswire) | Jun 18, 2026

Key Facts

Novo Nordisk announced pivotal Phase 3 data from the REIMAGINE programme for CagriSema, its combination injection. In a large global study of adults with type 2 diabetes, CagriSema produced significant reductions in both blood sugar (HbA1c) and body weight. It combines semaglutide (the GLP-1 agonist in Wegovy and Ozempic) with cagrilintide (an amylin analogue), and it beat semaglutide alone on both blood sugar control and weight loss. It's expected to reshape a metabolic market worth tens of billions.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Wegovy and Ozempic mimic one fullness hormone, GLP-1. CagriSema adds an amylin analogue to switch on a second pathway. People with type 2 diabetes are short on amylin as well as insulin, so CagriSema tops up both. Breaking through the ceiling of a single target by adding a second one is the whole point.

🎓 Career Lens

Most relevant for: Clinical Development, Medical Affairs, Regulatory Affairs

Asked about the future of obesity and diabetes drugs? CagriSema gives you something concrete to say:

"The field is moving from single GLP-1 drugs to combinations. CagriSema adds an amylin analogue to semaglutide and beat semaglutide alone on both HbA1c and weight in Phase 3. The questions I'd watch next are tolerability, since combining two drugs can mean more side effects, and how regulators and payers treat a combination product."

Why it matters: Combination therapies are complex to develop and file, which means more work, and more roles, in clinical, regulatory and medical teams.

STORY 3 · BIG PHARMA DEAL · ONCOLOGY

After Five Months of Secret Talks, Pfizer Signs a $10B Cancer Deal With Innovent

Fierce Biotech / Pfizer · Innovent Biologics (HKEX: 1801) | Jun 21, 2026

Key Facts

Pfizer struck a co-development deal worth $10 billion with Chinese biotech Innovent Biologics for its cancer pipeline, after five months of negotiations kept almost entirely out of view. For Pfizer, it's about quickly securing next-generation oncology assets before its 2027–2028 patent cliff, when major blockbusters lose protection. Innovent is strong in antibodies and immuno-oncology, and the deal gives it a springboard into global markets.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

A patent cliff is like a supermarket learning its best-selling products can't be sold after next year. Pfizer needs to restock the shelves fast, and in-house R&D won't be quick enough, so it paid $10 billion for a pipeline that's already moving. Five months of negotiation shows how urgent that is; big deals typically take much longer to agree.

🎓 Career Lens

Most relevant for: Business Development / Licensing, Corporate Development, Alliance Management

BD interviews often ask why Big Pharma keeps doing deals in China. Here's a way to answer:

"Pfizer faces a patent cliff in 2027 and 2028, and building a pipeline internally takes too long. Chinese biotechs like Innovent have strong antibody and immuno-oncology assets at a good stage, so a $10 billion co-development deal buys time. The five-month timeline tells you how urgent it was."

Why it matters: Cross-border deals like this need BD, legal and alliance people who understand both science and deal structure, and they're one of the fastest-growing corners of the industry.

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Three Ways Drugs Evolve

AI design, dual mechanisms, secret deals —
how do new medicines get to the next level?

① The question all three answer

Different methods, one question: how do you get past the limits of the old way? Insilico replaced manual screening with AI. Novo Nordisk expanded from mimicking one hormone to combining two. Pfizer broke through the speed limit of in-house R&D by bringing in an outside pipeline straight away. All three are moving away from drug development that's slow, single-target and done only in-house.

② Three methods, side by side
Insilico × SK Biopharm Novo Nordisk CagriSema Pfizer × Innovent
The Method
AI design — molecules built to fit the target Dual mechanism — GLP-1 plus amylin Fast deal — outside pipeline in five months
Key Result

Open Question
Will AI-designed molecules work in people? Tolerability and how payers treat it Whether the assets justify the price

③ Why now — the pressure behind all three

There's structural pressure behind all three. Single-target drugs have clear limits: semaglutide alone can't solve blood sugar, weight and complications all at once, which is why combinations are rising. Big Pharma's patent cliffs cluster in 2027–2029, so companies that don't bring in outside pipelines now face a revenue gap, hence Pfizer's speed. And generative AI in drug design has reached the point where Big Pharma and investors are placing real bets on it. For smaller biotechs, all three trends are openings: combination pipelines, AI partnerships and out-licensing are all more attractive than ever.

④ Career takeaway: understand the mechanism, then the money

✓ Skill to build

This week's thread is that mechanism literacy is becoming a commercial skill. You need to understand what an AI platform does, or why two hormones beat one, to judge a $2.5 billion or $10 billion deal. Even if you're not a scientist, learning how AI discovery works (target → generated molecule → virtual screening) and practising reading data on two targets at once will set you apart in BD, clinical and regulatory interviews alike.

⚠ Trap

Assuming "AI-designed" means "will succeed in the clinic." Most of Insilico's $2.5 billion is milestones, and the link between virtual success and clinical success is still limited. The same goes for "dual mechanism means better": interactions, side effects and a more complex regulatory path all matter. Read the data before the theme.

💡 Editor's Insight

The word that kept coming back to me this week was time. AI cuts the time to find candidates, combinations cut the time for patients to see results, and Pfizer even cut the time it took to negotiate. With patent cliffs looming, time really is money in this industry. But what got faster is the front end. The long tunnel of clinical trials is still there, and I don't want to forget that.

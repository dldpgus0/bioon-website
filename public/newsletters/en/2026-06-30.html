🔤 Word of the Week

Ex Vivo vs In Vivo Cell Therapy

Ex Vivo vs In Vivo Cell Therapy

The concept behind the key difference between this week’s CARsgen gastric cancer CAR-T (Story 1) and Moderna’s mRNA-6007 (Story 2)

Ex vivo means taking a patient’s cells out of the body, editing their genes in the lab, then infusing them back. Most CAR-T therapies so far (Kymriah, Yescarta and others) work this way. In vivo, by contrast, doesn’t take cells out at all: genetic instructions packed in mRNA or a viral vector are injected, and cells inside the body convert themselves. Moderna’s mRNA-6007 is applying exactly this approach to autoimmune disease.

💡 Analogy: Ex vivo is ‘custom-made in a factory, then shipped.’ Your cells go to the factory (the lab) to be modified for weeks before you get them back. In vivo is ‘assembled on site’ — send the parts (mRNA instructions) into the body and the cells convert themselves. Shipping time and cost disappear, but the key challenge is proving that on-site assembly works exactly as intended.

Ex Vivo

Like Kymriah and Yescarta, cells are collected, edited in the lab and re-infused. This week CARsgen’s gastric cancer CAR-T (Story 1) won the world’s first solid-tumour approval this way. It’s a proven approach, but weeks of manufacturing and costs in the hundreds of thousands of dollars are the barriers.

In Vivo

Like Moderna’s mRNA-6007, mRNA in LNPs is injected directly into the body to convert T cells on site. It aims for an ‘off-the-shelf’ model with no cell collection and immediate dosing — an access revolution. It’s still early in development, but it’s being watched as the next generation of cell therapy.

📰 This Week’s Top 3 Stories

Story 1 · Drug Approval

The World’s First Solid-Tumour CAR-T Approval — China’s NMPA Approves CARsgen’s Gastric Cancer Cell Therapy Satricabtagene

Fierce Pharma · CARsgen Therapeutics (6CAR.HK) | 2026.06.24

Key Facts

China’s National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) approved CARsgen Therapeutics’ satricabtagene autoleucel for advanced gastric cancer. It’s a historic first: the world’s first formal approval of a CAR-T cell therapy for a solid tumour. Until now, CAR-T only worked in blood cancers (leukaemia, lymphoma); in solid tumours, a special defensive shield called the tumour microenvironment (TME) made it extremely hard for CAR-T cells to get in. Satricabtagene broke through this barrier by targeting CLDN18.2, which is overexpressed on gastric cancer cells.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Existing CAR-T specialised in catching cancer cells floating in the blood (liquid cancers). A solid tumour is like a walled fortress: no matter how well-trained the immune soldiers (CAR-T) you send, getting inside was extremely hard. The therapy CARsgen just got approved found the key to the fortress’s front gate — using a distinctive protein found on gastric cancer cells (CLDN18.2) as a GPS to get in.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

Korea has one of the world’s highest rates of gastric cancer, so this approval is a direct signal of hope for Korean patients too. Korean cell therapy companies such as Curocell have new reason to strengthen solid-tumour CAR-T pipelines, and the MFDS could speed up talks on review standards for solid-tumour cell therapies. How global trials expand off the back of the NMPA approval is another point to watch.

📌 Satricabtagene autoleucel, an autologous CAR-T cell therapy targeting CLDN18.2, set a historic milestone as the world’s first solid-tumour CAR-T approval, in advanced gastric cancer. — Summary of Fierce Pharma

Story 2 · Clinical Pipeline

Moderna Unveils an In Vivo CAR-T for Autoimmune Disease — mRNA-6007 Deletes Lupus B Cells Directly Inside the Body

Fierce Biotech · Moderna (NASDAQ: MRNA) | 2026.06.26

Key Facts

Moderna unveiled mRNA-6007, an in vivo CAR-T program for autoimmune disease. It uses the mRNA–lipid nanoparticle (LNP) technology proven in its COVID vaccine to program T cells directly inside the body with a single injection, targeting and deleting the malfunctioning B cells in severe autoimmune diseases such as lupus. Ex vivo CAR-T takes cells out and edits them in the lab for weeks; mRNA-6007 aims to skip all of that with an ‘off-the-shelf’ product that can be given immediately.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Existing CAR-T is ‘send your cells to a factory for custom manufacturing, then get them back’ — weeks of time and hundreds of thousands of dollars. mRNA-6007 is ‘sending the factory line itself into the body.’ Like the COVID vaccine, mRNA instructions packed in lipid nanoparticles (tiny fat droplets) are injected, and T cells in the body convert themselves into CAR-T. The key is that no per-patient manufacturing is needed.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

Hundreds of thousands of people in Korea live with autoimmune diseases such as lupus and rheumatoid arthritis, and many severe patients aren’t controlled by existing biologics. If in vivo CAR-T becomes practical, it promises far lower cost and wider access than ex vivo. Moderna’s strategy of extending LNP technology could also be an important compass for Korean mRNA technology companies (such as ST Pharm).

📌 mRNA-6007 is an in vivo CAR-T approach that uses an mRNA-LNP platform to program T cells directly in the body and target autoimmune B cells — one of the boldest extensions of LNP technology since the pandemic. — Summary of Fierce Biotech

Story 3 · Phase 2 Trial

Obesity Drugs in Pill Form — Corxel’s Oral GLP-1 Candidate Shows Competitive Weight Loss in Phase 2

Fierce Biotech · Corxel Pharmaceuticals | Phase 2 | 2026.06.23

Key Facts

Corxel’s oral small-molecule GLP-1 candidate delivered competitive weight loss and blood sugar control in a Phase 2 trial. The key point is achieving efficacy comparable to today’s gold-standard injectables (Wegovy, Zepbound) in pill form. The technical hurdle for oral GLP-1 is that GLP-1 analogues are easily broken down by stomach acid; Corxel got around this with a small-molecule strategy. It’s a molecular race that could reshape the metabolic disease market dominated by Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Today’s best-selling obesity drugs (Wegovy, Zepbound) are injections. Many patients delay or stop treatment because ‘they don’t like needles.’ If an oral GLP-1 delivers the same effect, the market could grow several times over. It’s like smartphones replacing landlines — if performance is similar, convenience decides the market. Corxel’s Phase 2 data is a sign that possibility is getting closer to reality.

🇰🇷 The Korea Angle

Just as Korean reimbursement talks for obesity drugs get serious, an oral option would greatly expand the pool of potential patients. For Korean companies researching oral small-molecule GLP-1s, such as Dong-A ST, Corxel’s Phase 2 data is both a benchmark and competitive pressure. Timing the entry of Korean products for when oral obesity drugs gain reimbursement will be a key strategic question.

📌 An oral small-molecule GLP-1 candidate showed competitive results in both weight loss and blood sugar control in Phase 2 — potentially a turning point in the race to bring obesity drugs as pills instead of injections. — Summary of Fierce Biotech

🔬 This Week’s Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Two Futures for Cell Therapy

Ex vivo vs in vivo —
how a solid-tumour approval redrew the cell therapy map

① Why solid tumours were CAR-T’s graveyard

While CAR-T achieved remarkable results in blood cancers, it kept failing in solid tumours, for three reasons. First, the tumour microenvironment (TME) — solid tumours are surrounded by signals that suppress immune cells (PD-L1, TGF-β and others), so CAR-T cells quickly lose function even if they get in. Second, no clear target — blood cancers have clear markers found only on cancer cells, like CD19, but solid tumours share many antigens with normal cells, making it hard to pick what to attack. Third, physical barriers — it’s hard for T cells to actually push into dense tumour tissue. CARsgen found a target overexpressed in gastric cancer, CLDN18.2, and broke through all three.

② Ex vivo vs in vivo — two paradigms of cell therapy
Item Ex Vivo In Vivo
Example CARsgen satricabtagene (gastric cancer, world’s first solid-tumour approval) Moderna mRNA-6007 (lupus, early development)
How it’s made Collect cells → edit in the lab → re-infuse (takes weeks) Inject mRNA in LNPs → T cells convert inside the body (immediate)
Cost and access Hundreds of thousands of dollars / custom-made → low access Off-the-shelf possible → could fall dramatically

③ Why this distinction matters — access is the next competitive edge

CARsgen’s approval is ‘proof of possibility’: regulators have recognised that CAR-T can work in solid tumours. But commercial success depends on access. However effective it is, an ex vivo CAR-T costing hundreds of thousands of dollars reaches only a limited number of patients. That’s exactly what Moderna’s mRNA-6007 is aiming at — bringing cell therapy’s efficacy down to vaccine-level access. For K-bio, this trend is an opportunity too. If Korean CMOs like Samsung Biologics and SK bioscience build manufacturing capacity for LNP-based mRNA medicines, they could benefit from the era of in vivo cell therapy.

④ Career takeaway — what to prepare for in the cell therapy boom

✓ Skill to build

CMC (manufacturing and quality) and regulatory science (RA) in cell and gene therapy (CGT) are among the fastest-growing roles right now. Understanding the ex vivo CAR-T manufacturing process (cell collection, culture, quality testing), LNP formulation technology and solid-tumour trial design (how to assess the TME) sets you apart in interviews straight away. For clinical roles, it’s worth studying MFDS and FDA CGT regulatory trends for cell therapy review.

⚠ Trap

‘Approved in China’ doesn’t mean ‘approved globally.’ An NMPA approval is valid only in China; FDA and EMA approvals need separate clinical data and review. CARsgen’s approval is historic, but there’s still a long road to global commercialisation. The habit of reading approval scope region by region is essential in BD, investment and regulatory careers alike.

🔤 Word of the Week

Ex Vivo vs In Vivo Cell Therapy

The key difference between this week's CARsgen CAR-T (Story 1) and Moderna's mRNA-6007 (Story 2)

Ex vivo means taking a patient's cells out of the body, editing them in a lab and infusing them back. Almost every CAR-T on the market so far (Kymriah, Yescarta and others) works this way. In vivo means leaving the cells where they are: genetic instructions packaged in mRNA or a viral vector are injected, and the body's own cells convert themselves. Moderna's mRNA-6007 applies this approach to autoimmune disease.

💡 Analogy: Ex vivo is made-to-order in a factory, then shipped: your cells go to the lab for weeks of work and come back. In vivo is assembled on site: the instructions (mRNA) are sent into the body and the cells convert themselves. No shipping time or cost, but proving that on-site assembly works precisely is the big challenge.

Ex vivo

Like Kymriah and Yescarta, cells are collected, edited in a lab and reinfused. CARsgen's gastric cancer CAR-T (Story 1) won the first solid-tumour approval this way. Proven, but weeks of manufacturing and very high costs are barriers.

In vivo

Like Moderna's mRNA-6007, mRNA in lipid nanoparticles is injected directly and T cells are converted in the body. The aim is an off-the-shelf treatment with no cell collection, a big leap in access. Still early, but seen as the next generation of cell therapy.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · REGULATORY APPROVAL · CELL THERAPY

China Approves CARsgen's satri-cel, the World's First CAR-T for a Solid Tumour

Fierce Pharma · CARsgen Therapeutics (HKEX: 2171) | Jun 24, 2026

Key Facts

China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) approved CARsgen Therapeutics' satricabtagene autoleucel (satri-cel) for advanced gastric cancer. It's the first time a CAR-T cell therapy has been formally approved for a solid tumour anywhere in the world. Until now CAR-T only worked in blood cancers (leukaemia, lymphoma); in solid tumours, the tumour microenvironment (TME) made it extremely hard for CAR-T cells to get in and keep working. satri-cel targets CLDN18.2, a protein overexpressed on gastric cancer cells.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

CAR-T was built to catch cancer cells floating in the blood. A solid tumour is more like a fortress: even well-trained immune soldiers (CAR-T cells) struggle to get inside. CARsgen's therapy found a key to one of the fortress gates, using a protein that's especially abundant on gastric cancer cells (CLDN18.2) as its GPS.

🌍 Global Lens

A world-first approval coming from China's NMPA, not the FDA or EMA, is a milestone in itself. It shows Chinese regulators and companies can now set the pace in cell therapy. The open question is whether the data will convince Western regulators, who have historically wanted trials that include their own patient populations before approving.

Why it matters for your career: Cell therapy teams need people who can separate a regulatory milestone from global commercial reality — and CMC and regulatory roles in this area are among the fastest-growing in the industry.

📌 satri-cel, an autologous CAR-T targeting CLDN18.2, set a historic milestone as the world's first approved CAR-T for a solid tumour, in advanced gastric cancer. — Fierce Pharma summary

STORY 2 · PIPELINE · AUTOIMMUNE

Moderna Unveils mRNA-6007, an In Vivo CAR-T Designed to Wipe Out Lupus B Cells Inside the Body

Fierce Biotech · Moderna (NASDAQ: MRNA) | Jun 26, 2026

Key Facts

Moderna unveiled mRNA-6007, an in vivo CAR-T programme for autoimmune disease. It uses the mRNA–lipid nanoparticle (LNP) technology proven in its COVID vaccine to programme T cells inside the body with an injection. The target is the misbehaving B cells in severe autoimmune diseases such as lupus. Ex vivo CAR-T takes weeks of lab editing; mRNA-6007 aims to skip all that and be an off-the-shelf treatment that can be given straight away.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Conventional CAR-T is "send the cells to a factory for custom work and get them back" — weeks of time and a very high price. mRNA-6007 is more like sending the factory line itself into the body. As with the COVID vaccine, mRNA instructions in lipid nanoparticles (tiny fat droplets) are injected and T cells turn themselves into CAR-T cells. No patient-by-patient manufacturing needed.

🌍 Global Lens

In vivo CAR-T is one of the most crowded new races in biotech, with companies in the US, Europe and China all trying to make CAR-T cells inside the body. If it works, the biggest impact could be in places where personalised cell manufacturing has never been affordable, which is most of the world. That's the real global promise: cell therapy without the cell factory.

Why it matters for your career: Formulation and LNP expertise is moving from vaccines into therapeutics, and people who understand both are in demand.

📌 mRNA-6007 uses the mRNA-LNP platform to programme T cells in the body against autoimmune B cells — one of the boldest extensions of LNP technology since the pandemic. — Fierce Biotech summary

STORY 3 · PHASE 2 · OBESITY

Obesity Drugs in Pill Form: Corxel's Oral GLP-1 Shows Competitive Weight Loss in Phase 2

Fierce Biotech · Corxel Pharmaceuticals | Phase 2 | Jun 23, 2026

Key Facts

Corxel's oral small-molecule GLP-1 candidate showed competitive weight loss and blood sugar control in Phase 2. The point is efficacy that could stand up to today's gold-standard injections (Wegovy, Zepbound), in a pill. The technical problem with oral GLP-1s is that peptide GLP-1 drugs break down easily in stomach acid; Corxel got around it with a small-molecule approach. It's a new challenger in a market dominated by Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Today's best-selling obesity drugs are injections, and plenty of patients delay or stop treatment because they don't like needles. If an oral GLP-1 matches their effect, the market itself could get much bigger. Think of the smartphone replacing the landline: when performance is similar, convenience decides. Corxel's Phase 2 data suggests that's getting closer.

🌍 Global Lens

Many of the oral small-molecule GLP-1 candidates now in the clinic were first developed in China, and Western companies have been licensing them in. For global health, pills matter a lot. They're easier to ship, store and scale than injections that need a cold chain, which could make obesity treatment realistic in countries where injectables never will be.

Why it matters for your career: Small biotechs with Phase 2 obesity assets are exactly where BD teams look for deals, which makes this a live area for BD and commercial roles.

📌 An oral small-molecule GLP-1 showed competitive weight loss and blood sugar control in Phase 2 — a possible turning point in the race to bring obesity drugs to pills. — Fierce Biotech summary

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Two Futures for Cell Therapy

Ex vivo vs in vivo —
how a solid-tumour approval redraws the map

① Why solid tumours were a graveyard for CAR-T

While CAR-T did remarkable things in blood cancers, it kept failing in solid tumours, for three reasons. The tumour microenvironment is full of signals (PD-L1, TGF-β and others) that switch immune cells off. Clean targets are rare: blood cancers have markers like CD19, but solid tumours share many antigens with normal tissue. And there's a physical barrier, because T cells struggle to push into dense tumour tissue. With CLDN18.2, CARsgen showed, at least in gastric cancer, that the wall can be climbed.

③ Why it matters — access is the next battleground

CARsgen's approval is a proof of possibility: regulators accepted that CAR-T can work in a solid tumour. Commercial success, though, depends on access. An ex vivo CAR-T costing hundreds of thousands of dollars limits who can get it, however well it works. That's exactly what mRNA-6007 is going after: cell therapy efficacy at something closer to vaccine-level access. For manufacturers building LNP and mRNA capacity, the in vivo era could be a big opportunity.

④ Career takeaway: follow the bottleneck

✓ Skill to build

All three stories point to the same shift: the science is moving from "can it work?" to "can it reach enough patients?" The people who solve that are in manufacturing, formulation, regulatory science and market access. If you're interested in cell and gene therapy, learning how CAR-T is actually manufactured (collection, expansion, release testing) or how LNPs are formulated will set you apart faster than another course on the biology.

⚠ Trap

Reading "approved in China" as "approved globally." An NMPA approval only covers China; FDA and EMA approvals need their own data and review. CARsgen's approval is historic, but global commercialisation is still a long road. Always check which regulator approved what.

💡 Editor's Insight

If I had to sum up this week in one word, I'd pick access. The question used to be "is this even possible?" Now it's becoming "how many people can it reach, at what cost, and how fast?" That's why it was so interesting to see a very expensive cell therapy win a historic approval in the same week as attempts to get there with one injection or one pill. See you next issue. 🙏

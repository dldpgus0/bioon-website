🔤 Word of the Week

Surrogate Endpoint

vs. overall survival — the key difference between ivonescimab's final data (Story 1) and the basis for camizestrant's approval (Story 2)

What a trial ultimately needs to show is simple: how long, and how well, patients live. Measuring that directly is overall survival (OS), a hard endpoint. But OS can take years to confirm, so drugs are often approved first on a surrogate endpoint, such as tumour shrinkage or progression-free survival (PFS), that's known to track with survival. The catch: the link isn't perfect. A better surrogate doesn't guarantee better survival.

💡 Analogy: OS is the final exam: you only get the grade after the whole term. A surrogate endpoint is the mid-term. Do well in the mid-term and you'll probably pass the final, but it isn't guaranteed. This week, ivonescimab has its final exam result. Camizestrant was admitted on its mid-term.

Hard endpoint — overall survival (OS)

Measures how long patients actually live. Slow to confirm, but the most reliable evidence there is. This week's ivonescimab result is this kind.

Surrogate endpoint — response rate, PFS

A marker linked to survival that can be confirmed much sooner, getting drugs to patients faster. Whether it turns into a real survival benefit needs confirming after approval. Camizestrant was approved this way.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · IMMUNO-ONCOLOGY · FINAL SURVIVAL DATA

David vs Goliath in Lung Cancer: Summit and Akeso's Bispecific Beats Keytruda on Final Survival

Fierce Pharma · Summit Therapeutics (NASDAQ: SMMT) × Akeso (HKEX: 9926) | Sep 13, 2026

Key Facts

At the World Conference on Lung Cancer (WCLC 2026) in Seoul, Summit Therapeutics and Akeso presented final overall survival (OS) data from the Phase 3 trial of their PD-1×VEGF bispecific ivonescimab. Median OS in advanced NSCLC reached 30.8 months, versus 22.6 months with Keytruda. It's the confirmed ending to the interim story we covered in #15, now backed by the strongest kind of clinical evidence. Keytruda is the world's best-selling drug, with more than $25B in annual sales.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

An interim readout says "the trend looks good." Final OS says "patients actually lived longer." Tumour shrinkage or slower progression are encouraging, but they don't always turn into a survival benefit. Ivonescimab has now cleared that final, hardest test, which makes it the most credible Keytruda challenger so far.

🌍 Global Lens

The data was presented in Seoul, and the drug was born in China, so Asia is increasingly where the lung cancer story gets told. The big questions now are for regulators outside China and for payers everywhere: will the FDA, EMA and MHRA accept the evidence, and how do you price a challenger to the world's best-selling drug? Keytruda's key patents also start expiring later this decade.

Why it matters for your career: Endpoint literacy, knowing what each type of result can and can't prove, is exactly what clinical development, medical affairs and regulatory roles screen for.

STORY 2 · DRUG APPROVAL · REGULATORY SURPRISE

Against the Advisory Committee: FDA Approves AstraZeneca's Oral SERD for Hormone-Driven Breast Cancer

Fierce Biotech / BioSpace · AstraZeneca (NASDAQ/LSE: AZN) | Sep 8, 2026

Key Facts

The FDA approved AstraZeneca's next-generation oral SERD (selective oestrogen receptor degrader) camizestrant for ER-positive advanced breast cancer. What makes it notable is that the decision went against the FDA's own advisory committee, which had voted negatively. The FDA approved on surrogate-endpoint data rather than confirmed overall survival. Taken as a daily pill, camizestrant breaks down the oestrogen receptor inside tumour cells, rather than blocking hormone production further upstream.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

A negative advisory committee vote usually means "the definitive evidence isn't there yet." The FDA approved anyway because the surrogate signal was large and the unmet need is high. Approvals like this normally come with a condition: confirmatory data after approval. In effect: use it now, prove it for certain later.

🌍 Global Lens

Regulators don't always agree on surrogate endpoints. The EMA has often asked for stronger proof of real clinical benefit, and NICE judges cost-effectiveness separately, so an FDA approval that went against its own advisers doesn't guarantee the same result in Europe or the UK. For an Anglo-Swedish company, that gap is a real commercial question.

Why it matters for your career: Reading an FDA approval letter alongside an advisory committee briefing, and understanding why they can disagree, is core regulatory affairs work.

📌 An approval its advisory committee voted against — a new precedent for flexibility on surrogate endpoints. — Fierce Biotech / BioSpace summary

STORY 3 · PIPELINE EXPANSION · PAEDIATRIC OBESITY

Oral Wegovy Resolves Obesity in 40% of Teenagers in a Phase 3 Trial

Fierce Pharma · Novo Nordisk (NYSE: NVO) | Sep 8, 2026

Key Facts

Novo Nordisk released Phase 3 data for oral Wegovy (semaglutide) in adolescents with obesity: about 40% of participants reached a healthy BMI and were no longer clinically obese. It isn't a new mechanism; it's an effect already proven in adults, extended to a new age group. As a pill rather than an injection, it may also help teenagers stick with treatment.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Proof in adults doesn't automatically carry over to children: growing bodies handle drugs differently, which is why a dedicated trial was needed. The big message is cutting off cardiovascular problems before they start, but giving a still-growing child a drug for many years is a separate safety question that needs longer follow-up.

🌍 Global Lens

Childhood obesity is rising in most regions, including the UK, Asia and the Middle East, so paediatric data matters well beyond the US. But attitudes to putting teenagers on long-term medication vary a lot between countries, and each regulator will make its own call. Expect requests for longer safety follow-up before broad use.

Why it matters for your career: Paediatric extrapolation, deciding how much adult evidence carries over, is a specialised skill that's in growing demand as obesity drug makers move into younger patients.

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Not All Evidence Is Equal

A win on final data, an approval on a surrogate, a promise extended to teenagers —
three "success" headlines, three grades of proof

① Same word, different grade of proof

Every headline this week reads as a win, but the evidence behind each one is far from equal. Summit and Akeso won on the strongest evidence there is, final OS. AstraZeneca won approval on surrogate data that the advisory committee voted against. And Novo Nordisk extended an already-proven adult effect to teenagers; it didn't prove anything new about the mechanism. Read the fine print before you give each one the same weight.

③ Why it matters — not "did it win" but "what did it win on"

Headlines use the same words, "approved," "won," "succeeded," but what's underneath differs. A win on final OS (Summit and Akeso) is about as settled as clinical evidence gets. An approval on a surrogate (AstraZeneca) is closer to a conditional deal that can be reversed if confirmatory data disappoints. An adult effect extended to teenagers (Novo Nordisk) is a new claim that needs its own proof. Lump them together as "all wins" and your read on risk will be wrong.

④ Career takeaway — read the grade of evidence

✓ Skill to build

Get into the habit of asking "what was the actual endpoint?" whenever you read a results headline. Overall survival, a surrogate like PFS or response rate, or an extension to a new population each carries different weight. Trial design, biostatistics and reading regulatory documents (advisory committee briefings, approval letters) are becoming core skills across regulatory affairs, clinical development and medical affairs, not specialist extras.

⚠ Trap

Treating every "approved" or "succeeded" headline as equally solid. A surrogate-based approval is conditional and can be withdrawn if confirmatory data disappoints — accelerated approvals have been withdrawn before. Extending to a new population is a new claim, not an automatic extension of adult data.

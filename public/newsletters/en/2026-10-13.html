🔤 Word of the Week

Rebate

a.k.a. after-the-fact discount; in Korea, also a word for illegal kickbacks

The same word means opposite things depending on where you work. In US pricing, a rebate is a legitimate after-the-fact discount: you pay full price first and get money back later, which is the mechanism behind the 340B rebate pilot in Story 3. In Korea's pharma industry, "rebate" (리베이트) means an illegal payment to a doctor or hospital to win prescriptions, the kind of influence the Cochrane review in Story 2 looks at. One word, two legal worlds, and an easy trap in a global interview.

💡 Analogy: Think of money off at the till versus cashback you claim afterwards. A till discount costs you less up front; cashback means paying in full and waiting, so your cash is tied up in the meantime. The 340B rebate model works like cashback.

Upfront discount — "money off at the till" 🏷️

Eligible hospitals pay the already-discounted price when they buy. Cash isn't tied up and settlement is simple, but manufacturers have long said they can't easily check whether the same drug was discounted twice.

Rebate — "pay first, we'll refund you" 💸

The hospital pays the full price, submits claims data, then receives the discount back. Manufacturers can see the claims and screen out duplicate discounts, while hospitals say their cash is tied up and the paperwork grows.

STORY 1 · BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT · ASIA-PACIFIC LICENSING

Ono Bets $80M Upfront on Biohaven's IgG Degraders, Securing Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia Rights

Fierce Biotech · Ono Pharmaceutical × Biohaven | Oct 8, 2026

Key Facts

Ono Pharmaceutical is paying Biohaven $80 million upfront, with a $20 million milestone and a 20% profit share, for regional rights to three IgG-degrading drugs: the Phase 3 Graves' disease candidate BHV-1300 and the follow-on molecules BHV-1310 and BHV-1320. The territory covers Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and the ASEAN countries. IgG degraders clear the harmful antibodies that drive disease, and for Graves' and other antibody-driven autoimmune conditions this points to a self-injected autoinjector that could eventually reach Asian markets often served late by US-first developers.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Graves' disease is driven by antibodies that over-stimulate the thyroid, and today it is usually managed with anti-thyroid drugs, radioiodine or surgery. An IgG degrader goes after the antibodies themselves, which is a different approach. For Ono, buying regional rights to candidates a US biotech has already advanced is faster than building from scratch. For Biohaven, which cut back R&D in March, selling regional rights helps fund late-stage trials. It is still a Phase 3 asset, so efficacy and safety are not yet proven.

🌍 Global Lens

This is a growing pattern in which US biotechs fund late-stage work by carving out Asian territories. A single regional partner at least makes coordinated planning across regulators such as Japan's PMDA and Korea's MFDS conceivable, though Ono hasn't said how it will handle Korean approval or launch. For health systems across Southeast Asia, regional licensing can bring access earlier than a global launch sequence would.

Why it matters for your career: BD and licensing teams need people who can structure territory carve-outs and milestone and profit-share terms, and regional launch planners who can sequence Japan–Korea approvals.

STORY 2 · PHARMA MARKETING · PROMOTIONAL COMPLIANCE

The Price of a Free Lunch: Cochrane Review Links Pharma Perks to Costlier, Less Appropriate Prescribing

Fierce Pharma · Cochrane Review | Oct 9, 2026

Key Facts

A Cochrane review of 93 studies, most of them from the US, concluded that pharma payments and gifts increase inappropriate prescribing and the number of prescriptions, and probably raise costs. Across seven drug classes, the more free meals a doctor received, the more likely they were to prescribe the promoted product. Conflict-of-interest policies that limit these contacts probably improve how appropriately doctors prescribe.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

It sounds small, but a lunch here and there adds up to a different prescribing pattern. For patients and payers, that means the drug chosen may be the more heavily promoted one rather than the more suitable or cheaper one. Expect regulators and transparency campaigners to cite the review under the US Open Payments regime, which publishes payments to doctors, and under the UK's ABPI Code, enforced by the PMCPA.

🌍 Global Lens

The evidence is mostly American, but the levers are global: Open Payments in the US, the ABPI Code and PMCPA in the UK, and in Korea a ban on illegal rebates that punishes both giver and receiver, plus spending-reporting rules. Commercial teams everywhere should expect a stronger push away from hospitality-led field tactics and towards compliant medical education and outcome-measured omnichannel engagement.

Why it matters for your career: Compliance, medical affairs and commercial excellence teams need people who can design and audit promotion that stands up to scrutiny, and who can measure omnichannel engagement by outcomes.

📌 A free lunch turns out not to be free: patients and payers pick up the bill. — Fierce Pharma summary

STORY 3 · MARKET ACCESS · PRICING & REIMBURSEMENT

Hospitals Return to Court to Halt HHS's 340B Rebate Pilot Before January Launch

Fierce Healthcare · AHA × HHS/HRSA | Oct 9, 2026

Key Facts

The American Hospital Association, the Maine Hospital Association and three community providers sued HHS and HRSA in federal court in Maine to block the relaunched 340B Rebate Model Pilot. It covers 21 drugs from 10 manufacturers and would replace upfront discounts with after-the-fact rebates from January 1. The AHA puts the administrative burden at more than $1 billion a year, against HRSA's own estimate of $537.2 million. Safety-net hospitals say waiting for rebates would squeeze the cash they use to serve low-income patients, in a program whose purchases topped $100 billion in 2025.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

340B lets safety-net hospitals buy outpatient drugs at steep discounts. What changes here is the timing of the discount. Manufacturers want claims-level visibility to stop duplicate discounts, for example when a 340B discount and a Medicaid rebate land on the same drug, while hospitals worry about cash tied up as they wait. The court's decision will help determine whether manufacturers finally get the visibility they have long sought.

🌍 Global Lens

340B is a US-only programme, but together with the IRA and MFN-style policies such as last issue's GLOBE model it is one of the main forces on US net price. Any company selling in the US, including European and Asian firms, has to model gross-to-net with rebate timing in mind, and that includes teams planning US launches from the UK or Korea.

Why it matters for your career: Market access, pricing and contracting teams need people who can model gross-to-net and rebate flows, and who understand how claims data and administrative cost shape policy.

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Same Money, Different Flow

Who pays first, who gets paid later, who ends up holding the leverage —
three stories asking the same question from different seats

① Three stories, one flow of money

Read separately, these are an Asia licensing deal, a study of sales practices and a US pricing lawsuit. Laid together, they are all about the direction and timing of money. Ono takes on risk early by paying upfront, perks flow quietly towards prescribing decisions, and in 340B the fight is over whether the discount comes before or after the sale. Change the order and the plans, and the behaviour, change with it.

③ Why it matters — the order of money is the order of power

Whoever receives cash early gets breathing room but gives up rights, and whoever pays first carries the risk but gains exclusivity. Hospitality used to be an area nobody audited; now the evidence is piling up. In a rebate model, the side holding the cash holds the leverage. Ask who pays first and you can see who bears the risk and who has the power. Next time a headline number appears, look at the sequence as well as the size.

④ Career takeaway — jobs that read the flow of money

✓ Skill to build

If you're heading into BD and licensing, study how upfront, milestone and profit-share terms are built and how territories get carved up. In compliance and marketing, follow the move to evidence-based medical education and omnichannel engagement. In market access and pricing, learn net price (gross-to-net) and rebate settlement. Across all three, being able to explain the direction and timing of money in numbers is what stands out in an interview.

⚠ Trap

Lumping "confirmed money" and "possible future money" together. In this deal the only confirmed figure is the $80M upfront; the milestone and profit share carry conditions (the same lesson as the biobucks discussion in #18). Likewise a single study does not become law overnight, so keep what is decided separate from what is merely expected.

💡 Editor's Insight

What stuck with me after all three was the order, not the amounts. The same money carries different risk and different leverage depending on whether it goes first or arrives later. Next time you meet a big number, ask "who pays this, when, and on what conditions?" The headline gets much clearer. See you next issue. 🙏

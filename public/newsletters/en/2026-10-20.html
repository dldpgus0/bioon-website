🔤 Word of the Week

CHMP positive opinion

a.k.a. the last gate before EU approval

To be approved in Europe, a medicine first needs a recommendation from the CHMP, the EMA's expert committee, and then a legally binding decision from the European Commission (EC). A CHMP positive opinion is not the finish line; it is the last gate just before it. The EC usually follows the recommendation (typically within about two months), but nothing can be sold until that final decision is made.

💡 Analogy: Think of a driving test: the CHMP is the examiner who tells you that you passed, and the EC is the licensing office that issues the licence. A pass notice doesn't put you on the road; the licence does.

CHMP positive opinion — "we'd approve it" 🗳️

The EMA's expert committee reviews the efficacy and safety data and makes a recommendation. It isn't legally binding, but it is a strong signal that approval will follow.

EC approval — "you may now sell it" ✅

The European Commission turns the recommendation into a legal decision. Only then can the product be sold in the EU, and national pricing and reimbursement talks follow separately.

STORY 1 · OBESITY DRUGS · COMMERCIAL LAUNCH & COMPETITION

Swap the Jab for a Pill? Lilly's Foundayo Clears Europe's Key Hurdle

Fierce Pharma · Eli Lilly (NYSE: LLY) | Oct 16, 2026

Key Facts

The EMA's expert committee (CHMP) has recommended approving Eli Lilly's once-daily GLP-1 pill Foundayo (orforglipron) for adults with obesity or overweight who also have a weight-related health problem, and for type 2 diabetes. In obesity trials, patients lost about 5% to 11% of their body weight over 72 weeks, depending on dose, compared with 2% to 2.5% on placebo. The European Commission still has to give final sign-off.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

For people who put off weekly injections, a daily pill lowers the barrier to starting and staying on treatment, and could reach people who have never used a GLP-1. Commercially it is a two-brand race: Novo's Wegovy pill launched in the US three months earlier and leads with about 173,500 weekly prescriptions, against about 60,000 for Foundayo (Oral GLP-1 Tracker). Lilly is closing the gap with self-pay prices from $149 a month, so Europe will test whether a second-to-market brand can win on price, access and distribution.

🌍 Global Lens

The CHMP opinion applies to the EU, but approval, pricing and reimbursement then play out market by market, so the same pill can launch at different prices and speeds across Europe. Regulators elsewhere, such as the MFDS in Korea, make their own decisions, and the article gives no timeline outside Europe.

Why it matters for your career: Obesity franchise, market access and distribution teams need people who can plan a second-to-market launch on price, access and channel strategy.

STORY 2 · BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT · CHINA STRATEGY

Can You Still Buy Chinese Biotech? Beijing Floats a Foreign-Takeover Ban

Fierce Biotech · MOFCOM | Oct 16, 2026

Key Facts

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) has proposed changes to its export-control rules, open for comment until November 15, that would ban foreign companies from acquiring proprietary cell and gene therapy, siRNA and pathogen gain-of-function platforms. Out-licensing and outsourced research or manufacturing services would still be allowed, so most of the "buy from China" deals we've covered could carry on as they are.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Start with the numbers. Foreign deals for Chinese biotech assets reached $120 billion by August 2026, up 36% year on year, and more of the new medicines in Western pipelines now start in Chinese labs. Yet only 5 of 121 cross-border Chinese out-licensing deals in 2026 included an ownership transfer, and a Chinese law-firm partner called the draft "significantly better than market expectations". Deals like AstraZeneca's roughly $1 billion purchase of Gracell would likely be blocked.

🌍 Global Lens

For Western and Asian buyers alike, "license, don't acquire" looks like the realistic default. For other hubs such as Korea, Japan and Europe, it also means competing for the same Chinese-originated assets through licences rather than takeovers. Remember it is a draft in a consultation period, not a final rule.

Why it matters for your career: BD, legal and consulting teams need people who can structure licence versus acquisition deals and run diligence with China's commerce ministry.

📌 China hasn't said "we won't sell"; it has said "not the whole thing". — Fierce Biotech summary

STORY 3 · MARKET ACCESS · PRICING & REIMBURSEMENT

Hospitals vs Drugmakers, the Discount War: AbbVie and BMS Join the 340B Fight

Fierce Healthcare · AHA × HHS | Oct 15, 2026

Key Facts

The American Hospital Association and other plaintiffs sued HHS on October 9 to block HRSA's second 340B Rebate Model Pilot, which covers 21 drugs from 10 manufacturers and is due to start on January 1. AbbVie, BMS and Boehringer Ingelheim moved to join the case on October 14, and PhRMA, the industry's trade group, followed on October 15. Safety-net hospitals say switching from upfront discounts to after-the-fact rebates would cost them more than $1 billion a year in administration (HRSA estimates $537.2 million), money they say would come out of care for low-income patients in a program whose purchases topped $100 billion in 2025.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

This is last issue's "discount now versus rebate later" fight with the manufacturers now in the room. Drugmakers want rebates so they can use claims data to police duplicate discounts; hospitals want to protect their cash flow. In short, it's about who controls the money between list price and net price in one of America's biggest discount channels. As drugmakers want rebates, they appear to be joining on the side of keeping the pilot, and the ruling will shape contracting, forecasting and payer-mix modelling for 2027 launches.

🌍 Global Lens

340B is a US-only programme, but it sits alongside the IRA and MFN-style policies as one of the main forces on US net price. Any company selling in the US, whether headquartered in Europe, the UK or Asia, will have to rework its gross-to-net assumptions depending on how this resolves.

Why it matters for your career: Market access and pricing teams need people who can model gross-to-net under rebate scenarios and explain how hospital and distributor contracts would change.

📌 Hospitals and drugmakers have now met in the same courtroom. — Fierce Healthcare summary

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · When the Rules Change

Latecomers on price, blocked buyers on structure, rebate payers on maths —
three places where the rules of the game moved this week

① Rules are strategy

Read separately, these are a pill recommendation, a Chinese regulatory draft and a US pricing lawsuit. Laid together, they are all about how to win inside rules that have just changed. Lilly is chasing first-mover Novo on price, access and distribution, Beijing leaves licensing open in place of acquisition, and in 340B drugmakers and hospitals have gone to court over the rebate structure. The goals stay the same (reach patients, secure good assets, protect net revenue); only the route changes.

③ Why it matters — the winners create value inside the new rules

Latecomers have to win on price and access, buyers who are blocked from takeovers on licence structure, and those facing rebates on net-price arithmetic. When rules change, people split into those who complain and those who redesign, and the ones whose value rises are usually the second group. Change doesn't remove opportunity; it changes which skills are needed.

④ Career takeaway — turning rules into structures

✓ Skill to build

If you're drawn to obesity commercial roles, study how a second-to-market brand competes on price, access and distribution. In BD, learn the structural differences between acquisition, licence and outsourcing, plus diligence with China's commerce ministry. In market access, get comfortable with gross-to-net and rebate scenarios. In every case, being able to explain which routes remain when the rules change is what sets you apart in an interview.

⚠ Trap

Reading a "recommendation" or a "draft" as if it were final. The CHMP opinion still needs EC sign-off, and the Chinese text is a draft open for comment until November 15. Keep the tone of the headline ("clears", "bans") separate from the actual stage.

After reading all three, the idea that the first mover wins looked a little different. Latecomers find a route on price, blocked buyers on structure, and those facing rebates on arithmetic.

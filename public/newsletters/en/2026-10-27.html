Buy it, extend it or build it?
Three companies, three growth plans

Last issue was about rules changing. This week it's about where companies look for their next growth engine. Celltrion, built on biosimilars, licensed a preclinical antibody. AstraZeneca added a fifth indication to Ultomiris, a drug it already sells. AbbVie took a pipeline candidate through phase 3, hit the primary endpoint and still fell short of what the market wanted. 🔤 Word of the Week

Lifecycle management

a.k.a. growing a marketed drug's value and lifespan

Lifecycle management means keeping a drug's value and sales life growing after launch, through new indications, new formulations or dosing routes, and new patient groups. Developing a brand-new drug takes a lot of money and time, while a drug with a safety record can often be extended more efficiently, so large pharma companies have long used this approach. This week's Ultomiris reached its fifth US indication by moving from rare complement diseases into IgA nephropathy.

💡 Analogy: Think of it as adding dishes to a restaurant that already does well, rather than opening a new one. You have regulars (existing safety data) and less risk than a new site, but every new dish still has to prove customers like it (clinical trials).

New indication — "same drug, new disease" 🎯

A drug that is already approved shows benefit in another disease or patient group, and its label widens. Ultomiris adding IgAN is an example.

New drug — "new medicine, from scratch" 🔥

Discovery, preclinical work and phase 1 to 3 trials all have to be done from the start. It takes more time and money, and the risk of failure is higher.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT · LICENSING

From Biosimilars to New Drugs? Celltrion Licenses Kaigene's KG013 for Up to $580.5M

Fierce Biotech · Celltrion × Kaigene | Oct 22, 2026

Key Facts

Celltrion has licensed KG013, a preclinical autoimmune antibody, from Maryland-based Kaigene in a deal worth up to $580.5M. The upfront payment was not disclosed, and Kaigene is also eligible for milestones plus royalties on net sales. KG013 targets APRIL, a signalling protein involved in plasma cell survival and immunoglobulin production, with the aim of cutting the production of disease-causing antibodies. Its first indication is IgA nephropathy (IgAN), and Kaigene keeps an option to co-market it in South Korea.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Celltrion built its business on biosimilars, the copycat versions of blockbusters such as Remicade, Rituxan and Humira, and in recent years it has been moving into innovative medicines through antibody licensing deals. Last November it agreed to pay up to $744M for Kaigene's FcRn inhibitor KG006 and dual-acting antibody KG002, with $8M upfront. This deal follows the same formula: secure preclinical candidates early, then pay as results arrive. From a management view, it is a company using strengths it already has, in manufacturing, development and commercialisation, to buy its next growth engine.

🌍 Global Lens

IgAN is especially common in Asian populations: the company cites about 30,000 patients in South Korea, against about 160,000 diagnosed in the US. Kaigene's CEO said Celltrion's global manufacturing, development and commercialisation infrastructure was part of why it was chosen as a partner.

Why it matters for your career: BD and licensing teams need people who can screen preclinical assets and structure milestone-heavy deals, and portfolio strategy and finance teams need people who can value assets this early.

STORY 2 · PRODUCT STRATEGY · REGULATORY

One Drug, Five Diseases? AstraZeneca's Ultomiris Wins Accelerated FDA Approval in IgA Nephropathy

Fierce Pharma · AstraZeneca press release | Oct 21, 2026

Key Facts

On 21 October AstraZeneca's rare-disease unit Alexion announced FDA accelerated approval for Ultomiris (ravulizumab) to reduce proteinuria in adults with primary IgA nephropathy (IgAN) at risk of disease progression. In a prespecified interim analysis of the phase 3 I CAN trial, urine protein fell by 46.6% on Ultomiris and 5.6% on placebo at 34 weeks, a placebo-adjusted effect of 43.4%. It is Ultomiris's fifth US indication.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Accelerated approval rests on proteinuria, a surrogate marker, not on proof that the drug slows kidney function decline. That is still to be shown: I CAN's eGFR result at week 106 is meant to support full approval, and this path is common in IgAN. The market is already crowded, with Calliditas's Tarpeyo, Travere's Filspari and Novartis's Fabhalta on sale and more candidates waiting. Ultomiris brings a different mechanism as a C5 complement inhibitor, while Fabhalta is also a complement inhibitor but targets Factor B. Alexion joined AstraZeneca in a $39B acquisition in 2020, and Ultomiris made $4.7B in global sales in 2025, up 19% at constant currency. Adding indications to a drug that already sells is the textbook case of lifecycle management.

🌍 Global Lens

Reviews are also under way in Japan and other countries, and the sources give no Korean timeline. AstraZeneca planned to present late-breaking I CAN results at ASN Kidney Week on 23 October.

Why it matters for your career: Product strategy and brand teams plan which indications to pursue and in what order, and development and medical teams design the confirmatory trials that accelerated approvals depend on.

📌 Instead of inventing another drug, keep adding diseases to the one that already sells. — Fierce Pharma summary

STORY 3 · CLINICAL DEVELOPMENT · MARKET EXPECTATIONS

A Phase 3 Win That Still Disappoints: AbbVie's Lutikizumab Hits Its Endpoint, and a Rival Falls 35%

Fierce Biotech · AbbVie (NYSE: ABBV) | Oct 22, 2026

Key Facts

AbbVie's lutikizumab, which targets IL-1α and IL-1β, met the primary endpoint in the phase 3 Intrepid trial in hidradenitis suppurativa (HS). The trial randomised 1,400 patients to the subcutaneous drug or placebo. At week 16, 31.4% on lutikizumab reached HiSCR75 (at least a 75% reduction in abscesses and inflammatory nodules), against 23.4% on placebo. The result was statistically significant, but the 8.0-point gap was well short of phase 2, which showed up to 45.9% against 17.5%.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

The market reacted at once. H.C. Wainwright analysts had set the bar for competitive differentiation at 45% to 50% HiSCR75 with a gap of at least 25 points over placebo, and lutikizumab came nowhere near either. Shares in Avalo, which is developing an IL-1β antibody, fell 35% to $8 in early trading. BMO was calmer: the safety profile is clean, all eight ranked secondary endpoints were hit, including pain and flares, and it could offer an alternative mechanism. Rivals include Merck's tulisokibart (up to 27% placebo-adjusted in phase 2b) and UCB's Bimzelx (20%). AbbVie will report 36-week extension data in 2027 and plans to file in the second half of that year.

🌍 Global Lens

The article has no Korean angle, but the lesson travels to every market: a readout is judged against rivals' data and investors' expectations, not only against placebo.

Why it matters for your career: Competitive intelligence, market research and investor relations teams build the benchmarks a result is judged against, and clinical development teams need to set expectations with them from the start.

📌 A win on the primary endpoint is not the same as a win in the market. — Fierce Biotech summary

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Buy, Extend or Build: Three Growth Plans

Celltrion bought, AstraZeneca extended and AbbVie built.
Three ways to find the next growth engine in one week

① Three roads to the next growth engine

Read separately, these are a licensing deal, a rare-disease approval and a phase 3 readout. Laid together, they are three answers to where the next revenue comes from. Celltrion added an outside candidate to the capabilities it built on biosimilars (buy). AstraZeneca added another indication to Ultomiris, the drug it got through its $39B Alexion deal (extend). AbbVie took a pipeline candidate to phase 3 and found the market wanted more (build). The roads differ, but in all three the outcome depends on how well expectations are managed.

③ Why it matters: read a growth plan together with expectations

Buying early is cheap, but the value only appears once the asset proves itself in trials. Extending a drug carries less risk because safety data already exist, yet every new indication has to face its own competitors. Building from the pipeline can succeed on the primary endpoint and still be marked down if the result is below the market's expectation. The same success can read as a very different report card depending on expectations and where rival drugs stand.

④ Career takeaway: explaining a growth strategy with numbers

✓ Skill to build

In BD, learn how to structure and value milestone-heavy deals for preclinical assets. In product strategy and brand roles, practise sequencing indications and designing the confirmatory trials behind an accelerated approval. In competitive intelligence and investor relations, learn how to set a benchmark against rival data and manage expectations around it. In every case, being able to explain a company's growth strategy with numbers and evidence is what stands out in an interview.

⚠ Trap

Reading words like "success", "$580.5M" and "approval" at face value. $580.5M is a maximum and the upfront was not disclosed, Ultomiris's approval is accelerated on a proteinuria marker so kidney-function proof is still to come, and lutikizumab hit its primary endpoint but not market expectations. Keep what is confirmed, what is conditional and what depends on expectations apart.

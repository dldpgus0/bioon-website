AriBio's AR1001 worked only in patients not on other Alzheimer's drugs, Novartis called Leqvio's outcomes trial a success without giving a number, and J&J paid $2.2B upfront for an eye drug before phase 3. What was actually shown?

BIO:ON Insight EN #27 · Dec 1, 2026 · AriBio's AR1001 · Novartis's Leqvio · J&J × Ollin

They all said it worked.
How much did they actually show?

AriBio's phase 3 for AR1001, Novartis's ORION-4 result for Leqvio and J&J's purchase of Ollin. This week's three stories, read through one question: what sits behind the word "success".

Last issue looked at the evidence the FDA needs before it approves a drug. This week showed the step before that: the moment a company announces its result. All three companies had good news. AriBio said its phase 3 met its goal, Novartis said Leqvio significantly cut heart attacks and strokes, and J&J said it had bought an eye drug with best-in-class potential. Follow the numbers, though, and the amount of evidence each one showed was very different. I wanted to read past the word "success" to the numbers attached to it.
— Editor · MSc Biotechnology & Pharmaceutical Management

🔤 Word of the Week

Topline Results

a.k.a. a trial's first report card

The key idea behind this week's Leqvio (Story 2) and AR1001 (Story 1) news

Topline results are the first summary a company releases when a trial ends. They usually say only whether the primary endpoint was met and whether any major safety issue appeared. Listed companies have to disclose news that could move the share price quickly, so the detailed numbers often follow months later at a congress or in a journal. This week Novartis said ORION-4 "significantly" reduced cardiovascular events but left the size of the reduction to the full results in the first half of 2027.

💡 An analogy: It's like checking your exam answers against the published answer key. You know you passed the grade boundary, but your actual mark and each paper's score come with the official results. Picking a university on your own estimate is risky, and so is valuing a drug on its topline alone.

Topline — "the conclusion first" 📣

Fast, and it tells you the direction. The size of the effect, subgroups and safety detail may be missing.

Full results — "all the numbers" 📊

Presented at a congress or published in a journal, with effect sizes, confidence intervals, subgroups and safety. This is what doctors, regulators and payers judge a drug on.

📰 This Week's Top 3 Stories

STORY 1 · PHASE 3 · ALZHEIMER'S

A Miss Overall, a Win in One Group? AriBio's Oral Alzheimer's Drug AR1001 Posts Split Phase 3 Results

AriBio press release · Biospectator · Korea Biomedical Review | Nov 16, 2026

Key Facts

On 16 November at the CTAD 2026 congress in Boston, South Korea's AriBio presented results from POLARIS-AD, the global phase 3 trial of its oral Alzheimer's drug AR1001. 1,535 people with early Alzheimer's in 13 countries took 30 mg once a day for 52 weeks. On the primary endpoint, CDR-SB (a score of thinking and daily function), the overall population differed from placebo by just -0.11 points (p=0.4015), which was not significant. In the 604 patients not taking an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor or memantine, the difference was -0.44 points (p=0.0266), a 31% slowing of decline. More patients stopped treatment because of side effects on the drug: 5.2% vs 1.7%.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

AR1001's active ingredient, mirodenafil, is the same molecule as Mvix, an erectile dysfunction drug SK Chemicals got approved in Korea in 2007. AriBio licensed it in 2011 and has been redeveloping it for dementia, a classic case of drug repurposing. It belongs to the same PDE5 inhibitor class as Viagra. Whether you call the result a win depends on the trial design. AriBio set two primary populations from the start, overall and monotherapy, and agreed that significance in either would count. So the company says the trial met its objective, while Endpoints News called the data "tepid". AriBio plans to discuss the results with the FDA.

🌍 Global Lens

Shares of Samjin Pharm, which holds the Korean manufacturing and sales rights and is AriBio's second-largest shareholder, traded 13.65% higher before the next morning's open (Newsway, 08:20 on 17 Nov). The bigger question is global: will the FDA accept a benefit shown only in patients not on standard drugs, when many real patients take them? The same question applies to any company that pre-specifies a subgroup as a primary population.

Why it matters for your career: Regulatory affairs teams negotiate what evidence the FDA will accept, biostatisticians design multiple-population analyses, and medical affairs explains congress data to doctors.

📌 "The 31% slowing of clinical decline at one year in the Monotherapy population supports our hypothesis." — Jai-Jun Choung, Chairman and CEO, AriBio

STORY 2 · PHASE 3 · CARDIOVASCULAR

Two Jabs a Year, Fewer Heart Attacks and Strokes? Novartis's Leqvio Meets Its ORION-4 Primary Endpoint

Novartis press release · Novartis (SIX: NOVN) × University of Oxford | Nov 23, 2026

Key Facts

On 23 November Novartis said the phase 3 ORION-4 trial of Leqvio (inclisiran), its cholesterol-lowering injection, met its primary endpoint. Over 16,000 people with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) were randomised. Added to standard care, Leqvio produced a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in major cardiovascular events (coronary heart disease death, heart attack, ischaemic stroke or urgent coronary revascularisation). Novartis did not say by how much. Full results are planned for the first half of 2027. The trial was led by the University of Oxford with the TIMI Study Group and co-sponsored by Oxford and Novartis.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Leqvio is an siRNA drug that cuts the liver's production of PCSK9, a protein that stops LDL cholesterol being cleared. Patients get one dose, another at three months, then one every six months from a healthcare professional. It is already approved in more than 100 countries, but on the basis that it lowers LDL. Lowering a number is not the same as preventing heart attacks and strokes, and ORION-4 is the first trial to answer that question. Novartis says it is the first outcomes study of an siRNA therapy in ASCVD. The drug is licensed from Alnylam, and a second outcomes trial, VICTORION-2 PREVENT, also reads out in 2027.

🌍 Global Lens

A drug given twice a year in a clinic moves the burden of adherence from the patient to the health system, which could change how chronic-disease drugs compete. For payers such as the NHS and US insurers, the size of the event reduction in 2027 will decide how much that convenience is worth.

Why it matters for your career: Clinical operations teams run outcomes trials of this size, investor relations and communications manage the gap between topline and full data, and market access builds the value case for payers.

📌 "ORION-4 marks a meaningful advance demonstrating for the first time the potential to significantly reduce cardiovascular risk with a twice-yearly therapy." — Ruchira Glaser, Head, Cardiovascular, Renal and Metabolism Development Unit, Novartis

STORY 3 · M&A · OPHTHALMOLOGY

$2.2B Upfront Before Phase 3? J&J to Buy Eye Disease Biotech Ollin for Up to About $3.3B

Johnson & Johnson press release · CNBC · J&J (NYSE: JNJ) × Ollin Biosciences | Nov 16, 2026

Key Facts

On 16 November J&J agreed to buy Ollin Biosciences, a Texas-based eye disease biotech, for $2.2B upfront plus up to about $1.125B in regulatory and commercial milestones, roughly $3.3B in total. The prize is OLN324, a bispecific antibody that blocks both VEGF and angiopoietin-2 (Ang2), aimed at wet age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular oedema, which together affect more than 40 million people worldwide. Separately, Blackstone Life Sciences will pay J&J $750M over several years to fully fund OLN324's phase 3, in return for milestones and single-digit royalties. The deal is expected to close before the end of 2026.

Plain-English Breakdown 🧩

Ollin was founded in 2023 and is backed by ARCH Venture Partners and Mubadala Capital. According to CNBC, Ollin says an early head-to-head study showed faster and greater improvements in retinal structure and greater vision gains than Roche's Vabysmo, but no numbers were given. Vabysmo also blocks VEGF and Ang2, so OLN324 is a follow-on built in a smaller protein format (a "minibody"). The money is the interesting part. As with Blackstone's funding of Pfizer's obesity drug in issue #25, a private investor is paying for phase 3 in return for a share of any success. J&J pays to own the drug, but shares the risk of its most expensive trial.

🌍 Global Lens

Eye injections are a big, competitive market where biosimilars of Regeneron's Eylea, including several from Korean companies, are already arriving. Big pharma is placing its bets on the next generation of bispecifics, and on financing deals that let it do so without carrying the full phase 3 bill.

Why it matters for your career: Business development and valuation teams price early data with no published numbers, ophthalmology clinical development designs the phase 3, and R&D finance structures deals like Blackstone's.

📌 "The data generated to date are compelling" — Kip Connor, Vice President, Ophthalmology Disease Area Stronghold Leader, Johnson & Johnson

🔬 This Week's Deep Dive

Deep Dive · Between the Announcement and the Evidence

Three pieces of good news,
three very different amounts of evidence

① Same tone, different amounts of data

All three announcements opened with positive words. AriBio published numbers for both populations, Novartis released only its conclusion, and J&J paid $2.2B upfront on early comparative data with no numbers attached. The more numbers a company releases, the more room for argument (AriBio); the fewer, the more room for hope (Novartis, J&J). The place to start is not the tone of a release but what it shows and what it leaves out.

② Three announcements, split three ways

Overall group not significant; will the FDA accept the subgroup? How big the reduction is, follow-up length Actual numbers, phase 3 results
When we find out
FDA talks; extension study ends Q3 2027 Full results H1 2027 Deal closes in 2026, then phase 3

③ Why it matters — a topline is a trailer, not the film

A topline tells you what to watch for next. If Leqvio's reduction is small, its main selling point stays convenience; if it is large, treatment guidelines could change. For AR1001, the same numbers could support an approval or trigger a request for another trial, depending on how the FDA views the monotherapy group. For OLN324, only a phase 3 win over Vabysmo justifies $2.2B. All three get their real report card in 2027.

④ Career angle — the people who read the release

✓ Skill to build

In clinical development or biostatistics, learn how many primary populations a trial had and how multiplicity was handled. In regulatory affairs, ask whether a subgroup result can persuade the FDA. In BD or investing, ask what early data with no numbers is worth. In any of these roles, being able to separate what a press release shows from what it leaves out stands out in interviews.

⚠ Trap

Drawing conclusions from headlines like "phase 3 success", "primary endpoint met" or "best-in-class potential". AR1001 was not significant overall, Leqvio has no effect size yet, and OLN324 hasn't started phase 3. You can only size a success once you know in whom, by how much and compared with what.

💡 Editor's Insight

While writing this issue I deleted and retyped the word "success" more times than I can count. AriBio's result met its objective by design but not across all patients, and Novartis says Leqvio worked but not yet by how much. I also jumped to conclusions from the headlines at first. Following the numbers, all three turned out to be stories that haven't been fully told yet. Next time you see "phase 3 success", ask: in which patients, and by how much? See you next issue. 🙏

Job ads alone rarely tell you why a role is becoming more important. But group the news by role and patterns appear. I re-read the 17 issues and 51 stories BIO:ON Insight covered from June to September 2026 and pulled out **four signals that came up again and again**.

I'm still a student learning the industry, so this isn't hiring data or an expert forecast — it's **the patterns I noticed while writing up the news**. Always check each company's careers page for actual openings.

## 1. The battleground moves to where drugs are made — CMC & quality

- Ultragenyx's Sanfilippo syndrome gene therapy Fayuvi was rejected once over manufacturing, then approved after fixes. ([Issue #17](/en/insight/2026-09-22))
- A batch of Fresenius's biosimilar was recalled over glass particles in the vials. ([Issue #12](/en/insight/2026-08-18))

**How to read it:** As more hard-to-make drugs like gene therapies arrive, proof that a drug can be made to the same quality every time is deciding approval and supply as much as efficacy.

**What to prepare:** GMP basics, the ideas of deviations and CAPA, and one example of why CMC affects approval.

## 2. Good drugs don't always get prescribed — Market access

- Lilly's oral obesity drug Foundayo got off to a slower start than its competitor, and coverage from the three biggest US PBMs only began in June, about two months after launch. ([Issue #8](/en/insight/2026-07-12), [Issue #9](/en/insight/2026-07-19))
- Later, amid an oral GLP-1 price war, Foundayo cut its cash price and took one in three new prescriptions. ([Issue #18](/en/insight/2026-09-29))
- With ultra-expensive drugs like a $3.95M gene therapy, how to justify the price and who pays keeps coming up. ([Issue #17](/en/insight/2026-09-22))

**How to read it:** After the science wins, the next fight is over coverage, price and channels.

**What to prepare:** The big picture of reimbursement, the idea of cost-effectiveness (ICER), and an example that answers "why isn't this good drug selling?"

## 3. Regulatory decisions get more transparent — and more nuanced — Regulatory affairs

- The FDA resumed publishing complete response letters (CRLs), making it harder for companies to spin rejections. ([Issue #9](/en/insight/2026-07-19))
- Twice-rejected oncolytic virus RP1 turned an advisory committee vote around with real-world data. ([Issue #11](/en/insight/2026-08-04))
- The FDA approved camizestrant despite a negative advisory vote, while in the same week ivonescimab strengthened its case with final OS data. ([Issue #16](/en/insight/2026-09-15))

**How to read it:** Approval isn't a simple pass/fail. It depends on the type and strength of evidence and on dialogue with regulators — and the more that's made public, the more regulatory communication matters.

**What to prepare:** Practise explaining CRLs, advisory committees and surrogate endpoints vs OS with an example each. The RA filter in the [interview question bank](/en/career/interview) helps.

## 4. People who read the structure behind the headline number — BD

- The Lilly × InnoCare deal was worth up to $3.35B, but only about 3% was guaranteed. ([Issue #18](/en/insight/2026-09-29))
- Roche paid $190M upfront, with up to $2.3B more in milestones, for Hanmi's 'muscle-sparing' obesity candidate HM17321. ([Issue #14](/en/insight/2026-09-01))
- Big acquisitions like GSK's ($10.6B) and AbbVie's ($10.9B) were strategies to fill the patent cliff. ([Issue #3](/en/insight/2026-06-15), [Issue #7](/en/insight/2026-07-06))

**How to read it:** Deals keep getting bigger and more frequent, but the yardstick for value is the upfront, milestone and royalty structure — not the total. Korean companies are increasingly the ones being licensed or acquired.

**What to prepare:** The difference between bio-dollars and guaranteed upfront, the patent cliff, and practice explaining one recent deal by its structure.

## The one thing all four share

All four signals come down to **the ability to read behind the headline**. If you can take one story and talk through fact → interpretation → your role, it should help in an interview for any role. The method is in my [three-step interview answer guide](/en/career/industry-news-interview).

I'll keep noting new patterns as I write the newsletter every Tuesday.

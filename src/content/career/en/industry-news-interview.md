One question shows up in almost every biotech and pharma interview: **"Is there any recent industry news you've followed closely?"** Many candidates stop at summarizing the story. What the interviewer actually wants to hear is **how you see the industry**.

## Three steps: fact → interpretation → your role

1. **Fact (one sentence)** — what happened, with one number.
2. **Interpretation (1–2 sentences)** — the structure behind the headline.
3. **Your role (1–2 sentences)** — what it means for the job you're applying for.

## Example: a licensing deal, answered for a BD role

Let's practice with the Lilly × InnoCare deal from BIO:ON Insight EN #7.

> **Fact:** Lilly signed a licensing deal with China's InnoCare worth up to $3.35B.
>
> **Interpretation:** But only $100M was guaranteed upfront — about 3% of the headline — with the rest tied to clinical and regulatory milestones.
>
> **Your role:** In BD, reading that structure correctly matters: when evaluating a Korean biotech's out-licensing deal, you look at guaranteed money and milestone conditions, not the headline total.

## Common mistakes

- **Stale news** — prepare something from the last one or two months.
- **Off-role topics** — a sales interview answered only with R&D detail feels disconnected.
- **Wrong numbers** — if you're unsure, say "roughly 3%" rather than guessing precisely.

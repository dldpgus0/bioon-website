One question shows up in almost every biotech and pharma interview: **"Is there any recent industry news you've followed closely?"** Many candidates stop at summarising the story. What the interviewer actually wants to hear is **how you see the industry — and whether that view fits the role**.

## Three steps: fact → interpretation → your role

1. **Fact (one sentence)** — what happened, with one number.
2. **Interpretation (one or two sentences)** — the structure behind the headline. Why does it matter?
3. **Your role (one or two sentences)** — what it means for the job you're applying for, and what you'd look at if you were in that seat.

Aim for 40–60 seconds. Linger on the fact and it becomes a recap; skip the interpretation and the link to your role feels thin.

## Worked examples by role

Every example below comes from real news covered in BIO:ON Insight. Don't memorise them — **borrow the structure** and put it in your own words.

### Business development — the Lilly × InnoCare licensing deal

> **Fact:** Lilly signed a deal with China's InnoCare worth up to $3.35B for five targets.
>
> **Interpretation:** But the guaranteed upfront and near-term payments were about $100M — roughly 3% of the headline. The rest depends on clinical and regulatory milestones, so the headline number and the real value are very different things.
>
> **Your role:** In BD, I'd judge any out-licensing deal by the upfront share and the milestone conditions first, not the total.

Source: [Issue #19](/en/insight/2026-09-29)

### Regulatory affairs — an FDA approval despite a negative advisory vote

> **Fact:** The FDA approved AstraZeneca's oral SERD camizestrant for breast cancer, despite a negative advisory committee vote.
>
> **Interpretation:** The committee only recommends; the FDA makes the final call on overall risk–benefit. In the same week, ivonescimab strengthened its case with final overall survival data — side by side, they show that 'approved' and 'strength of evidence' aren't the same thing.
>
> **Your role:** In RA, I'd want to agree early with regulators on which endpoints, and what level of evidence, a filing will rest on.

Source: [Issue #17](/en/insight/2026-09-15)

### Market access — an effective obesity pill that wasn't being prescribed

> **Fact:** Lilly's oral obesity drug Foundayo got off to a slower start in prescriptions than expected.
>
> **Interpretation:** The efficacy data was strong, but coverage from the three biggest US pharmacy benefit managers only started in June, about two months after launch. Before coverage, patients pay much more — so even a good drug struggles to pick up prescriptions quickly.
>
> **Your role:** Reimbursement plays the same role in Korea and elsewhere, so market access teams need value evidence and a pricing strategy ready well before approval.

Source: [Issue #9](/en/insight/2026-07-12)

### CMC & quality — a recall over glass particles

> **Fact:** Fresenius Kabi voluntarily recalled one batch of its IL-6 biosimilar after microscopic glass particles were found in the vials.
>
> **Interpretation:** It was a container problem, not an efficacy problem — but patient safety meant the whole batch came off the market. However good the molecule, a defect at the final fill-and-finish step can still pull a product.
>
> **Your role:** In quality, root-cause investigation (CAPA) and supplier control are what prevent this, and that's the work I want to contribute to.

Source: [Issue #13](/en/insight/2026-08-18)

## Common mistakes

- **Stale news** — prepare something from the last one or two months. Skimming the most recent issues before your interview is usually enough.
- **News unrelated to the role** — deep R&D mechanism detail won't land in a sales interview. The same story needs a different angle for each role.
- **Getting numbers wrong** — if you're unsure, give a range ("about 3%"). A wrong number costs trust instantly.
- **No answer to the follow-up** — "So what does that mean for us?" almost always comes next. Prepare one line linking the story to the company's pipeline or business.

## How to practise

1. Pick your target role and choose two or three recent issues tagged with it — the role filter in the [Insight archive](/en/insight) finds them quickly.
2. Write an answer for each using the three steps, then say it out loud in under 60 seconds.
3. Use the [interview question bank](/en/career/interview) to draw random questions for the same role and practise the follow-ups.

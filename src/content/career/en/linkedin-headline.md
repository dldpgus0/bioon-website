On LinkedIn, the first things a recruiter sees are your photo and your **headline**. It also appears right under your name in search results. Yet many students' headlines end at "Student at [University]". That causes two problems.

- **You don't show up in searches.** Recruiters search by role: "Regulatory Affairs", "CRA", "Business Development". If the role isn't in your headline, you're unlikely to appear.
- **Nobody can tell what you want to do.** Your university is already in the education section. The headline isn't the place to repeat it.

## The headline formula

**[Target role] | [2–3 degree or skill keywords] | [one differentiator]**

- **Target role** — use the standard English job title. That's what recruiters type.
- **Degree or skill keywords** — your subject, training you've completed (e.g. GCP), tools you can use. Pick words that appear in job ads.
- **Differentiator** — one thing you've actually done that others haven't: a project, an internship, a competition, a writing habit you've kept up.

Headlines can be up to 220 characters, but on mobile only the start shows — so **put the target role first**.

## Examples by role

- **CRA / clinical development:** `Aspiring CRA | Pharmacology BSc · ICH-GCP certified | Weekly clinical trial news analysis`
- **Regulatory affairs:** `Regulatory Affairs candidate | Pharmacy · FDA & EMA pathways | Regulatory writing`
- **Business development:** `Biotech Business Development | Pharmacology MSc · Licensing deal analysis | Publisher of a weekly biotech newsletter`
- **Market access:** `Market Access & Pricing | Pharmacy · HTA & reimbursement basics | Health economics coursework`
- **CMC & QA/QC:** `QA/QC & CMC | Biotechnology · GMP training · Biologics manufacturing internship`

## Building the 'thing you've done'

Your differentiator has to be true, and visible elsewhere on your profile. Without work experience, the fastest route is **a small, consistent activity**.

1. **Post one news story a week from your target role's angle** — three to five lines is enough. If you're aiming for BD, break down a licensing deal's structure; for RA, the reasons behind an approval or rejection.
2. **Back it up** — always include the source link and one key number, e.g. "of the $3.35B headline, about 3% is guaranteed."
3. **After four weeks, put it in your headline** — something like "Weekly biotech deal analysis" that describes what you've actually been doing.

## Checklist

- Is your target role, in standard English, at the very start?
- Are there **two or three keywords** from real job ads?
- Is there **at least one thing you've actually done**, beyond your degree?
- Can someone confirm your headline claims in your **experience, activity or posts**?
- Have you removed **adjectives nobody searches for**, like "passionate" or "hardworking"?

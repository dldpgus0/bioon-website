## How to use this

1. Download the **English CV template (.docx)** above. Replace anything in square brackets `[ ]`.
2. Run through the checklist below.
3. Bring your LinkedIn profile in line with it (last section).

If a company's application system asks for a particular file format (.docx or PDF), use that.

## 1. A format that gets through applicant tracking systems (ATS)

Many multinationals read applications with an ATS first. Software parses and sorts your CV before a person sees it, so a hard-to-parse layout can lose content.

- **Use a single column.** Avoid tables, text boxes, graphics, images and charts [1][2].
- **Keep the header and footer empty.** Put your name and contact details at the top of the main body [2].
- **Use standard section headings** such as Contact Information, Summary, Skills, Experience and Education. Creative headings like "My Journey" can confuse an ATS [1][2].
- Basic emphasis — bold, capitals, italics — is fine [2].

## 2. What UK and US CVs leave out

- **No date of birth and no photo**, unless you're applying for acting or modelling [3].
- Contact details: name, home address, mobile number and email [3].
- Korean companies often use their own application forms — when applying to one, follow its format (my own view).

## 3. Writing the content

- **Show achievements, not duties — with numbers where you can.** What changed because of your work? Start lines with active verbs such as 'created' and 'analysed', and avoid over-used phrases like 'team player' [3]. e.g. `Analysed 17 weekly biotech news issues and published role-specific takeaways for [N] subscribers` — every number must be true.
- **Work backwards from the job description.** Find what the employer values most, reflect it with the same language and examples, and put the most important achievements first. Embed keywords naturally in your achievement lines rather than stuffing them in [3].
- **Keep the profile at the top short:** two or three sentences on your education, experience and the role you want, tailored to the job [5].
- **Length:** one page early in your career, up to two pages with several years of experience [3].
- **For biotech roles, consider these sections (my own suggestion):** Technical Skills (lab techniques, data tools), Training & Certifications (GCP, GMP), Projects, Publications & Posters.

## 4. Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Single column, with no tables, text boxes or images
- [ ] Header and footer are empty
- [ ] Section headings are standard
- [ ] No date of birth or photo (for UK, US and multinational English applications)
- [ ] Keywords from the job ad appear alongside real experience
- [ ] Every number and achievement is true and you can explain it in an interview
- [ ] The file name is clear, e.g. `Firstname_Lastname_CV`

## 5. LinkedIn profile checklist

- [ ] **Headline:** follows `[target role] | [2–3 keywords] | [one differentiator]`. Up to 220 characters, and only the start shows on mobile [4]
- [ ] **About:** a slightly longer version of your CV Summary, with the target role and strengths in the first two lines
- [ ] **Experience:** uses the same achievement lines as your CV
- [ ] **Skills:** lists the skills from job ads first
- [ ] **Activity:** one industry story a week, written from your target role's angle

The headline formula and role-by-role examples are in BIO:ON Career's [LinkedIn headline post](/en/career/linkedin-headline).
